The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Utah''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the four [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|State]] of [[Utah]], one from all four of the state's [[Utah's congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections will coincide with the [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate elections|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. The primary elections took place on June 23, 2026.<ref>{{Cite web|date=May 9, 2025 |title=2026 State Primary Election Dates |url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates|website= NCSL |access-date=5 August 2025}}</ref>

== Background ==
[[File:Utah Congressional Districts, 120th Congress.svg|thumb|250px|Map of Utah's congressional districts as ordered by the Third District Court of Utah on November 10, 2025 and will be used starting at the 2026 House elections.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Schoenbaum |first=Hannah |date=November 11, 2025 |title=Judge adopts Utah congressional map creating a Democratic-leaning district for 2026 |url=https://apnews.com/article/utah-redistricting-congressional-map-democrats-a443a6584fad0adeeb5eadcc336a4390 |access-date=November 14, 2025 |work=[[Associated Press]]}}</ref>
<br>{{maplink|from=Utah's 1st congressional district (2027–).map|from2=Utah's 2nd congressional district (2027–).map|from3=Utah's 3rd congressional district (2027–).map|from4=Utah's 4th congressional district (2027–).map|text=Interactive map version}}
]]
The [[Utah Supreme Court]] ruled in July 2024 that the [[Utah State Legislature|state legislature]] had overstepped their constitutional authority when it repealed [[2018 Utah Proposition 4|Proposition 4]] in 2020 (and subsequently drew congressional districts that heavily favored Republican candidates).<ref>{{Cite news |last=Wines |first=Michael |date=2024-07-11 |title=Utah's Gerrymandered House Map Ignored Voters' Will, State Supreme Court Says |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/07/11/us/utah-supreme-court-gerrymandering.html |access-date=2025-04-06 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> [[League of Women Voters v. Utah State Legislature|The case]] was remanded to the district court to determine a final remedy. The decision was decided on August 25, 2025, ordering the new Utah congressional map be redrawn within 30 days.<ref>{{Cite web |last=McKellar |first=Katie |date=2025-02-01 |title=Will Utah get new congressional maps? Judge hears oral arguments in Utah redistricting case • Utah News Dispatch |url=https://utahnewsdispatch.com/2025/01/31/will-utah-get-new-congressional-maps-judge-hears-oral-arguments-in-utah-redistricting-case/ |access-date=2025-04-05 |website=Utah News Dispatch |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Aerts |first=Lindsay |date=April 1, 2025 |title=Judge over gerrymandering case against Utah legislature asks for clarification over tossing maps |url=https://www.abc4.com/news/politics/inside-utah-politics/judge-over-gerrymandering-case-against-utah-legislature-asks-for-clarification-over-tossing-maps/ |access-date=April 5, 2025 |website=[[KTVX|ABC 4 Utah]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250910102610/https://www.abc4.com/news/politics/inside-utah-politics/judge-over-gerrymandering-case-against-utah-legislature-asks-for-clarification-over-tossing-maps/ |archive-date=September 10, 2025 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-08-26 |title=Judge rules Utah's congressional map must be redrawn for the 2026 elections |url=https://apnews.com/article/utah-redistricting-congressional-map-gerrymandering-a6722505b8e76eda5c73fc346eadd9aa |access-date=2025-08-26 |website=AP News |language=en}}</ref> 

Utah's state legislature passed a new map on October 11, 2025, which created two competitive seats leaning slightly Republican. District judge Dianna Gibson enjoined the map passed by legislature on November 10, and selected a map drawn by civil groups as the final map, which contained a solidly Democratic seat centered around Salt Lake City.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-11-11 |title=Utah judge denies GOP-passed congressional map |url=https://www.politico.com/news/2025/11/11/utah-judge-denies-gop-passed-congressional-map-00646183 |access-date=2025-11-11 |website=POLITICO |language=en}}</ref>

The new district is based in and encompasses the majority of [[Salt Lake County, Utah|Salt Lake County]], including the cities of [[Salt Lake City]], [[West Valley City, Utah|West Valley]], [[Millcreek, Utah|Millcreek]], [[Cottonwood Heights, Utah|Cottonwood Heights]], and [[Murray, Utah|Murray]], as well as the [[Salt Lake City International Airport]] and the [[University of Utah]]. Due to redistricting, this is a district with no incumbent. In the [[2024 United States presidential election|2024 presidential election]], Democrat [[Kamala Harris]] won the district by a 24-point margin.<ref name="inside-elec">{{cite web |last1=Rakich |first1=Nathaniel |title=A Detailed Analysis of Utah’s New Congressional Map |url=https://www.insideelections.com/news/article/a-detailed-analysis-of-utahs-new-congressional-map |website=Inside Elections |access-date=May 10, 2025 |date=November 11, 2025}}</ref>

The new district is based in northern Utah, encompassing the counties of [[Box Elder County, Utah|Box Elder]], [[Cache County, Utah|Cache]], [[Rich County, Utah|Rich]], [[Davis County, Utah|Davis]], and a majority of [[Weber County, Utah|Weber]], and includes the cities of [[Logan, Utah|Logan]], [[Farmington, Utah|Farmington]], [[Ogden, Utah|Ogden]], and [[Brigham City, Utah|Brigham City]], as well as [[Hill Air Force Base]] and [[Weber State University]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Blake Moore]], who was re-elected in the 1st district with 63.1% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT">{{cite web|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|title=2024 House Vote Tracker|access-date=February 26, 2025|website=[[The Cook Political Report with Amy Walter|Cook Political Report]]}}</ref> In the [[2024 United States presidential election|2024 presidential election]], Republican [[Donald Trump]] won the district by a 29-point margin.<ref name="inside-elec"/>

The new district is based in eastern and southern Utah, encompassing a majority of the state's counties and portions of [[Utah County|Utah]] and [[Weber County, Utah|Weber]] counties, and includes the cities of [[Cedar City, Utah|Cedar City]], [[St. George, Utah|St. George]], [[Provo, Utah|Provo]], [[Price, Utah|Price]], [[Orem, Utah|Orem]], and [[Heber, Utah|Heber]], as well as [[Brigham Young University]].
The incumbent is Republican [[Celeste Maloy]], who was re-elected in the 2nd district with 58.0% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/> In the [[2024 United States presidential election|2024 presidential election]], Republican [[Donald Trump]] won the district by a 41-point margin.<ref name="inside-elec"/>

[[File:Utah's 4th congressional district (since 2027) (new version).svg|thumb|270px|Utah's 4th congressional district boundary from the 2026 elections
<br>{{maplink|from= Utah's 4th congressional district (2027–).map|text=Interactive map version}}
]]
The new district is based in western Utah, encompassing the counties of [[Tooele County, Utah|Tooele]], [[Juab County, Utah|Juab]], [[Millard County, Utah|Millard]], [[Sanpete County, Utah|Sanpete]], [[Sevier County, Utah|Sevier]], and portions of [[Salt Lake County]] and [[Utah County]], and includes the cities of [[Draper, Utah|Draper]], [[Sandy, Utah|Sandy]], [[Tooele, Utah|Tooele]], and [[South Jordan, Utah|South Jordan]]. Due to redistricting, the district has two incumbents: Republican [[Burgess Owens]], who was re-elected with 63.4% of the vote in 2024, and Republican [[Mike Kennedy]], who was elected in the 3rd district with 66.4% of the vote in 2024. Owens announced in March 2026 that he would not seek re-election.<ref name="HPVT"/><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.politico.com/live-updates/2026/03/04/congress/burgess-owens-retires-00813495|title=Utah Republican Burgess Owens announces he’ll retire at the end of this term|access-date=March 4, 2026}}</ref> In the [[2024 United States presidential election|2024 presidential election]], Republican [[Donald Trump]] won the district by a 32-point margin.<ref name="inside-elec"/>
