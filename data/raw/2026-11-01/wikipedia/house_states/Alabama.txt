The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Alabama''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the seven [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|state]] of [[Alabama]], one from all seven of the state's [[Alabama's congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections will coincide with [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate elections|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. The primary elections were held on May 19, and in races where no candidate received over 50% in a primary, runoff elections took place on June 16. Special primary elections were held on August 11 in districts that were affected by the new congressional map following redistricting in May.

This election will take place alongside races for [[2026 United States Senate election in Alabama|U.S. Senate]], [[2026 Alabama gubernatorial election|governor]], [[2026 Alabama Senate election|state senate]], [[2026 Alabama House of Representatives election|state house]], and [[2026 Alabama elections|numerous other state and local offices]].

==Redistricting and special primaries==
[[File:Alabama Congressional Districts, 120th Congress.svg|thumb|District lines passed by the [[Alabama Legislature]] in May 2026 after ''[[Louisiana v. Callais]]'' for the 2026 elections<ref name="LLS">{{Cite web |title=Alabama |url=https://redistricting.lls.edu/state/alabama/ |access-date=2026-06-19 |website=All About Redistricting |language=en}}</ref><br>({{maplink|from=Alabama's 1st congressional district (2027–).map|from2=Alabama's 2nd congressional district (2027–).map|from3=Alabama's 3rd congressional district (2027–).map|from4=Alabama's 4th congressional district (2027–).map|from5=Alabama's 5th congressional district (2027–).map|from6=Alabama's 6th congressional district (2027–).map|from7=Alabama's 7th congressional district (2027–).map|text=Interactive map version}})]]
Following a ruling by the [[Supreme Court of the United States]] that narrowed section 2 of the [[Voting Rights Act of 1965|Voting Rights Act]], [[Kay Ivey]] called state legislators in for a special session to allow for redrawing of Alabama's congressional map. In the session, a bill was passed to allow for the state to redraw the map if a previous ruling that had blocked the state from redrawing mid-decade following a previous lawsuit was lifted. The Supreme Court lifted that restriction on May 11, 2026, clearing the way for the state to redistrict.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Quinn |first1=Melissa |title=Supreme Court clears path for Alabama to redraw congressional map |url=https://www.cbsnews.com/news/supreme-court-clears-path-alabama-redraw-congressional-map/ |website=CBS News |access-date=12 May 2026 |date=May 11, 2026}}</ref> On May 12, Ivey scheduled special primary elections to be held in the 1st, 2nd, 6th, and 7th congressional districts on August 11, with no possible runoff election.<ref>{{cite web |title=2026 Special Primary Writ of Election |url=https://governor.alabama.gov/newsroom/2026/05/2026-special-primary-writ-of-election/ |website=The Office of Alabama Governor |access-date=12 May 2026 |date=May 12, 2026}}</ref> May 19 primary elections will still be held in the affected district, but the results will be nullified following the special primary.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Cason |first1=Mike |title=Gov. Kay Ivey calls second Alabama primary election in wake of Supreme Court ruling |url=https://www.al.com/politics/2026/05/gov-kay-ivey-calls-second-alabama-primary-election-in-wake-of-supreme-court-ruling.html |website=AL.com |access-date=12 May 2026 |date=May 12, 2026}}</ref>

A panel of federal judges blocked the new map on May 26. Soon after, the state appealed the ruling up to the Supreme Court,<ref>{{cite web |last1=Ashcraft |first1=Carter |title=Federal judges block Alabama congressional map ahead of special primaries |url=https://yellowhammernews.com/federal-judges-block-alabama-congressional-map-ahead-of-special-primaries/ |website=Yellowhammer News |access-date=26 May 2026 |date=May 26, 2026}}</ref> which on June 2 overturned the panel's decision, permitting Alabama to use the new map.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Barrett |first1=Anna |title=Supreme Court allows Alabama to use 2023 congressional map in August special primary |url=https://alabamareflector.com/2026/06/02/supreme-court-allows-alabama-to-use-2023-congressional-map-in-august-special-primary/ |website=Alabama Reflector |access-date=11 June 2026 |date=June 2, 2026}}</ref>

{{see also|Alabama's 1st congressional district}}
[[File:Alabama's 1st congressional district (since 2027) (new version).svg|thumb|Alabama's 1st congressional district boundary from the 2026 elections<br>{{maplink|from=Alabama's 1st congressional district (2027–).map|text=Interactive map version}}]]
In the original court-ordered map, this district encompassed the entirety of [[Baldwin County, Alabama|Baldwin]], [[Coffee County, Alabama|Coffee]], [[Covington County, Alabama|Covington]], [[Dale County, Alabama|Dale]], and [[Escambia County, Alabama|Escambia]] counties, including the cities of [[Bay Minette, Alabama|Bay Minette]], [[Daphne, Alabama|Daphne]], [[Enterprise, Alabama|Enterprise]], [[Ozark, Alabama|Ozark]] and majority white sections of [[Mobile, Alabama|Mobile]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Barry Moore (American politician)|Barry Moore]], who was elected with 78.4% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT">{{cite web|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|title=2024 House Vote Tracker|access-date=March 9, 2025 |website=[[The Cook Political Report with Amy Walter|Cook Political Report]]}}</ref>

In the legislature-approved redistricted map, the 1st district was redrawn to only include the southwestern part of the state near the Gulf of Mexico, encompassing all of both Mobile and Baldwin counties, as well as Escambia and Covington counties. Southeastern parts of the state in the Wiregrass Region were redrawn from the 1st district into the 2nd district.<ref name=arise>https://alarise.org/blog-posts/what-you-need-to-know-about-alabamas-aug-11-special-primary-election/</ref>

{{see also|Alabama's 2nd congressional district}}
[[File:Alabama's 2nd congressional district (since 2027) (new version).svg|thumb|Alabama's 2nd congressional district boundary from the 2026 elections<br>{{maplink|from=Alabama's 2nd congressional district (2027–).map|text=Interactive map version}}]]
In the original court-ordered map, the 2nd district encompassed all of [[Montgomery County, Alabama|Montgomery County]] and the capital city of [[Montgomery, Alabama|Montgomery]], as well as majority Black sections of the [[Wiregrass (region)|Wiregrass Region]] and the city of Mobile. It also included the entirety of [[Butler County, Alabama|Butler]], [[Macon County, Alabama|Macon]], [[Monroe County, Alabama|Monroe]], [[Pike County, Alabama|Pike]], and [[Russell County, Alabama|Russell]] counties. The incumbent is Democrat [[Shomari Figures]], who was elected with 54.6% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

In the legislature-approved redistricted map, the 2nd district was redrawn to include southeastern parts of the state in the Wiregrass Region that previously belonged to the 1st district, including Coffee County. It maintains Montgomery County but no longer stretches to majority Black areas of Mobile County.<ref name=arise/>
