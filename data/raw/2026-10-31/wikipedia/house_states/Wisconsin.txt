The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Wisconsin''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the eight [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|state]] of [[Wisconsin]], one from each of the state's eight [[Wisconsin's congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections will coincide with [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate elections|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. The primary election took place on August 11, 2026.
{{Horizontal TOC|nonum=yes|limit=2}}

==Background==
In July 2025, a lawsuit was filed by two liberal law firms in a Wisconsin district court, asking to consider the constitutionality of Wisconsin's congressional maps.<ref>{{cite web |date=2025-07-22 |title=New Lawsuit Alleges Wisconsin's Congressional Map Violates State Constitution |url=https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/wisconsin-congressional-map-discriminates-democrats-unconstitutional-lawsuit/ |access-date=2025-10-01 |website=Democracy Docket}}</ref> In September 2025, the Wisconsin Supreme court addressed this lawsuit and asked each party to provide briefs on whether this court case should proceed.<ref>{{cite web |last=Kremer |first=Rich |date=2025-09-25 |title=Wisconsin Supreme Court orders legal briefs in 2 congressional map challenges |url=https://www.wpr.org/news/wisconsin-supreme-court-orders-legal-briefs-in-2-congressional-map-challenges |access-date=2025-10-01 |website=WPR}}</ref> On March 31, 2026, a judicial panel dismissed the challenge.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Rego |first1=Max |title=Judicial panel dismisses challenge to Wisconsin congressional map |url=https://thehill.com/homenews/campaign/5809613-wisconsin-congressional-map-challenge/ |work=[[The Hill (newspaper)|The Hill]] |access-date=April 1, 2026 |date=March 31, 2026}}</ref>

The 1st district encompasses the southeastern corner of Wisconsin, containing the cities of [[Beloit, Wisconsin|Beloit]], [[Franklin, Milwaukee County, Wisconsin|Franklin]], [[Janesville, Wisconsin|Janesville]], [[Kenosha, Wisconsin|Kenosha]], [[Oak Creek, Wisconsin|Oak Creek]], [[Racine, Wisconsin|Racine]], and most of [[Whitewater, Wisconsin|Whitewater]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Bryan Steil]], who was re-elected with 54.0% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT">{{cite web|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|title=2024 House Vote Tracker|access-date=December 2, 2024 |website=[[The Cook Political Report with Amy Walter|Cook Political Report]]}}</ref>

The 3rd district takes in the [[Driftless Area]] in southwestern Wisconsin including [[Eau Claire, Wisconsin|Eau Claire]] and [[La Crosse, Wisconsin|La Crosse]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Derrick Van Orden]], who was re-elected with 51.4% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT">{{cite web|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|title=2024 House Vote Tracker|access-date=December 2, 2024 |website=[[The Cook Political Report with Amy Walter|Cook Political Report]]}}</ref>

The election was one of the most expensive congressional races in the 2026 elections, with over $31.6 million in spending.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Mutnick|first=Ally|date=October 6, 2026|title=The blockbuster House races|url=https://punchbowl.news/article/campaigns/blockbuster-house-races/|access-date=2026-10-15|website=Punchbowl News|language=en-US}}</ref>
