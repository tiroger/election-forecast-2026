{{Elections in Virginia}}
The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Virginia''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the 11 [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|state]] of [[Virginia]], one from each of the state's [[Virginia's congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections will coincide with [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate elections|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. Primary elections took place on August 4, 2026.<ref>{{Cite web|date=May 9, 2025 |title=2026 State Primary Election Dates |url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates|publisher=[[National Conference of State Legislatures]] |access-date=August 5, 2025}}</ref>

==Redistricting==
===Background===
In April 2026, voters narrowly approved a [[2026 Virginia redistricting amendment|constitutional amendment]] to allow for mid-decade redistricting and to instate a map that would have likely given Democrats 10 of Virginia's 11 seats in the [[United States House of Representatives|House of Representatives]].<ref>{{Cite news |title=Virginia votes yes on proposed congressional redistricting, AP projects|url=https://www.wtvr.com/news/virginia-politics/congressional-redistricting-constitutional-amendment-april-21-2026|date=April 21, 2026|publisher=[[WTVR-TV|WTVR]]}}</ref> However, on May 8, the [[Supreme Court of Virginia]] ruled the referendum vote null and void in a 4–3 decision, stating the [[Virginia General Assembly|General Assembly]] "violated the intervening-election requirement in Article XII, Section 1 of the [[Constitution of Virginia]]" by first passing the amendment while early voting was underway for the [[2025 Virginia elections|2025 elections]].<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Khalil|first1=Jahd|title=Supreme Court of Virginia nullifies redistricting vote results|url=https://www.vpm.org/generalassembly/2026-05-08/scova-redistricting-referendum-scott-mcdougle-kelsey|date=May 8, 2026|publisher=[[WCVE-FM|VPM]]}}</ref> As a result, the 2021 Special Masters map remained in effect for the [[2026 United States elections|2026 midterms]]. Following the ruling, House [[List of speakers of the Virginia House of Delegates|Speaker]] [[Don Scott (Virginia politician)|Don Scott]] and [[Virginia Attorney General|Attorney General]] [[Jay Jones]] filed an emergency appeal to the [[Supreme Court of the United States]] and filed a motion requesting the state Supreme Court to pause its ruling from taking effect while the appeal plays out.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Wilder |first=Drew |date=2026-05-08 |title=Virginia Democrats to appeal ruling against redistricting to U.S. Supreme Court  |url=https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/local/virginia-democrats-appeal-ruling-redistricting-us-supreme-court/4102136/?amp=1 |access-date=2026-05-08 |publisher=[[WRC-TV]] |language=en}}</ref> On May 15, the U.S. Supreme Court declined to hear the appeal.<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Howe|first1=Amy|title=Court denies Virginia's request to reinstate congressional map that would benefit Democrats|url=https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/05/court-denies-virginias-request-to-reinstate-congressional-map-that-would-benefit-democrats/|date=May 15, 2026|website=[[SCOTUSblog]]}}</ref>

{{see also|Virginia's 1st congressional district}}
The 1st district is based in the western [[Chesapeake Bay]] and includes portions of suburban [[Richmond, Virginia|Richmond]]. Within the district are western [[Henrico County, Virginia|Henrico]] and [[Chesterfield County, Virginia|Chesterfield]] counties. Other localities in the district include [[Colonial Beach, Virginia|Colonial Beach]], [[Mechanicsville, Virginia|Mechanicsville]], and [[Williamsburg, Virginia|Williamsburg]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Rob Wittman]], who was re-elected with 56.3% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT">{{cite web|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|title=2024 House Vote Tracker|access-date=December 2, 2024 |website=[[The Cook Political Report with Amy Walter|Cook Political Report]]}}</ref>

===Election background===
Congressman Wittman, first elected in the [[2007 Virginia's 1st congressional district special election|2007 special election]] for this seat, has not faced serious opposition from Democrats in previous election cycles. However, with President [[Donald Trump]] narrowly carrying the district while losing statewide by mid-single digits in the [[2024 United States presidential election in Virginia|2024 presidential election]], and Democrat [[Abigail Spanberger]] winning the district in the [[2025 Virginia gubernatorial election|2025 gubernatorial election]], Democrats now view Wittman as potentially vulnerable to a serious challenger. As a result, seven Democrats filed to seek the nomination against Wittman, with [[Henrico County, Virginia|Henrico County]] [[District attorney|Commonwealth's Attorney]] [[Shannon Taylor (attorney)|Shannon Taylor]] ultimately winning the nomination. Despite Wittman facing a serious challenge for the first time, most predictors expect the race to still favor the incumbent.<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Suter|first1=Tara|title=Taylor looks to unseat Wittman in key Virginia House race|url=https://thehill.com/homenews/campaign/6004695-taylor-wittman-virginia-house-race/|date=August 4, 2026|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=[[The Hill (newspaper)|The Hill]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|last1=Heckt|first1=Shannon|title=Wittman seeks to keep 1st District seat, as Democratic challengers face crowded primary|url=https://virginiamercury.com/2026/06/10/wittman-seeks-to-keep-1st-district-seat-as-democratic-challengers-face-crowded-primary/|date=June 10, 2026|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=[[Virginia Mercury]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=Virginia 1 2026 HOUSE FORECAST|url=https://www.pollsmax.com/2026-us-house/virginia-1/forecast/|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=PollsMax.com}}</ref>

{{see also|Virginia's 2nd congressional district}}
The 2nd district is based in [[Hampton Roads]], containing the cities of [[Chesapeake, Virginia|Chesapeake]], [[Franklin, Virginia|Franklin]], [[Suffolk, Virginia|Suffolk]], and [[Virginia Beach, Virginia|Virginia Beach]]. Virginia's [[Eastern Shore of Virginia|Eastern Shore]] is also located within the district. The incumbent is Republican [[Jen Kiggans]], who was re-elected with 50.7% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

===Election background===
Virginia's 2nd congressional district is one of the most competitive districts in the country. The district has a long history of ticket splitting, with voters supporting both Democrats and Republicans on the same ballot for different offices. For instance, in 2024, [[Donald Trump]] narrowly carried the district, while Democratic Senator [[Tim Kaine]] also carried it. In 2025, Democrats [[Abigail Spanberger]] and [[Ghazala Hashmi]] won the district in their respective races, while Republican [[Jason Miyares]] carried it in his. As a result, the district has a [[Cook Partisan Voting Index]] rating of EVEN.<ref>{{Cite web|title=2025 Cook PVI℠: District Map and List (119th Congress)|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/cook-pvi/2025-partisan-voting-index/district-map-and-list|access-date=2025-04-05|website=Cook Political Report|date=April 3, 2025 |language=en}}</ref> Congresswoman Kiggans successfully defended her seat in the [[2024 United States House of Representatives elections in Virginia|2024 election]]. Democrats subsequently nominated former Congresswoman [[Elaine Luria]], setting up a rematch of the [[2022 United States House of Representatives elections in Virginia|2022 election]], in which then State Senator Kiggans defeated Luria for the seat.<ref>{{Cite web|author=Daily News Headlines|title=Luria Easily Wins Democratic Primary in Virginia's Second District Will take on Kiggans in November election|url=https://shoredailynews.com/headlines/luria-easily-wins-democratic-primary-in-virginias-second-district-will-take-on-kiggans-in-november-election/|date=August 5, 2026|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=Shore Daily News}}</ref> This is expected to be one of the most closely watched races in the [[2026 United States elections|2026 midterms]].<ref>{{Cite magazine|last1=Segers|first1=Grace|title=Is This District the Key to the 2026 Midterms?|url=https://newrepublic.com/post/215092/elaine-luria-virginia-swing|date=September 3, 2026|access-date=September 5, 2026|magazine=[[The New Republic]]}}</ref>

In May 2026, Kiggans appeared on a [[radio show]] where the radio host, [[Rich Herrera]], criticized House minority leader [[Hakeem Jeffries]]'s efforts to [[2026 Virginia redistricting amendment|redraw the state's congressional districts]] saying, "get your cotton‑picking hands off of Virginia." Kiggans replied, "That's right. Ditto. Yes. Yes to that", receiving criticism from Democrats. Kiggans apologized and clarified her positions, in which she disagreed with the statement's [[Slavery in the United States|connection to slavery]] from the host.<ref>{{cite web|last=Hauari|first=Gabe|date=May 12, 2026|title=GOP lawmaker criticized after racist on-air remark|url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/05/12/rep-jen-kiggans-virginia-radio-remarks/90042789007/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260512182209/https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/05/12/rep-jen-kiggans-virginia-radio-remarks/90042789007/|archive-date=May 12, 2026|access-date=June 22, 2026|publisher=[[USA Today]]}}</ref>

In September 2026, Luria sued the [[Virginia Department of Elections]] in an attempt to remove independent candidate Bishop Staten from the ballot. In the suit, Luria alleges that, despite over 600 signatures having been declared invalid, an additional 18 should also be rejected, which would bring Staten under the required threshold to appear on the ballot. Staten is running on the [[Democratic Socialists of America]] platform, though the local DSA chapter denies any affiliation with him.<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Jarvis|first1=Brandon|title=Luria files lawsuit against Department of Elections questioning validity of Independent candidates' signatures|url=https://www.virginiascope.com/luria-files-lawsuit-against-department-of-elections-questioning-validity-of-independent-candidates-signatures/|date=September 4, 2026|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=Virginia Scope}}</ref> Staten has a long history of supporting President Trump.<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Jarvis|first1=Brandon|title=Independent candidate causes concern for Democrats in VA-02|url=https://virginiapoliticalnewsletter.substack.com/p/independent-candidate-causes-concern|date=August 24, 2026|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=Virginia Political Newsletter}}</ref>

{{see also|Virginia's 5th congressional district}}
The 5th district encompasses the majority of rural [[Southside (Virginia)|Southside Virginia]], including the cities of [[Charlottesville, Virginia|Charlottesville]], [[Danville, Virginia|Danville]], and [[Lynchburg, Virginia|Lynchburg]]. The incumbent is Republican [[John McGuire (Virginia politician)|John McGuire]], who was elected with 57.3% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

===Election background===
Despite the 5th district being a strong Republican bastion that has continued to back Republicans even amid double-digit statewide losses, such as in the [[2025 Virginia elections|2025 elections]], the [[Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee|DCCC]] has placed the district on its list of targeted seats to flip.<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Beyer|first1=Elizabeth|title=Notes from the Square: DCCC triples down on its push to flip the 5th Congressional District|url=https://cardinalnews.org/2026/08/21/notes-from-the-square-dccc-triples-down-on-its-push-to-flip-the-5th-congressional-district/|date=August 21, 2026|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=Cardinal News}}</ref> Congressman McGuire, a freshman member of the House, won this seat with 57.3% of the vote in the last election after narrowly ousting former Congressman [[Bob Good]] in a primary. Democrats nominated former Congressman [[Tom Perriello]], who held this seat for one term from 2009 to 2011. The race is expected to favor the freshman incumbent; however, predictors are hesitant to rate the seat as safe due to Perriello outraising McGuire.<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Yancey|first1=Dwayne|title=Perriello has nearly three times as much cash as McGuire; Macy outraises Cline|url=https://cardinalnews.org/2026/07/17/perriello-has-nearly-three-times-as-much-cash-as-mcguire-macy-outraises-cline/|date=July 17, 2026|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=Cardinal News}}</ref> If Perriello pulls the upset, he would be the fifth different representative for this seat in the last six election cycles. McGuire has refused to debate Perriello, stating he will not debate unless "he [Perriello] needs to go on national TV and apologize for accusing me of [[insider trading]]."<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Mullet|first1=Allison|title=Rep. John McGuire says an apology is needed before he debates challenger Perriello|url=https://wset.com/news/local/rep-john-mcguire-says-an-apology-is-needed-before-he-debates-politics-august-2026-perriello|date=August 27, 2026|access-date=September 13, 2026|website=[[WSET-TV]]}}</ref>

{{see also|Virginia's 6th congressional district}}
The 6th district is located in western Virginia taking in the [[Shenandoah Valley]] along [[Interstate 81]]. The district is anchored at the southern end by the cities of [[Roanoke, Virginia|Roanoke]] and [[Salem, Virginia|Salem]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Ben Cline]], who was re-elected with 63.1% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

===Election background===
Although Democrat [[Beth Macy]] has outraised Congressman Cline, the 6th district is even more Republican than its counterpart in the 5th. As a result, the [[Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee|DCCC]] initially had not place the seat on its targeted list of seats to flip, and Congressman Cline had not been expected to face a serious challenge.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Virginia 6 2026 HOUSE FORECAST|url=https://www.pollsmax.com/2026-us-house/virginia-6/forecast/|website=PollsMax.com}}</ref> However, on October 2, the DCCC added this district to its target list.<ref>{{Cite web|title=House Democrats grow more bullish about midterms as target list expands|url=https://rollcall.com/2026/10/02/house-democrats-target-list-midterm-elections-2/|website=rollcall.com}}</ref>

{{see also|Virginia's 7th congressional district}}
The 7th district is based in suburban, exurban, and rural areas of [[Northern Virginia|Northern]] and [[Greater Richmond Region|Central Virginia]]. The district contains  [[Bowling Green, Virginia|Bowling Green]], [[Culpeper, Virginia|Culpeper]], the city of [[Fredericksburg, Virginia|Fredericksburg]], [[Spotsylvania County, Virginia|Spotsylvania]], [[Stafford, Virginia|Stafford]], [[Stanardsville, Virginia|Stanardsville]], [[Woodbridge, Virginia|Woodbridge]], and a small sliver of [[Albemarle County, Virginia|Albemarle County]]. The incumbent is Democrat [[Eugene Vindman]], who was elected with 51.2% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

===Election background===
The [[National Republican Congressional Committee|NRCC]] has placed the 7th district on its list of seats to target and flip. Its strategy is to attempt to tie incumbent freshman Congressman Vindman to Governor [[Abigail Spanberger]] in hopes of stirring resentment among rural portions of the district over Democrats' failed [[2026 Virginia redistricting referendum|redistricting attempt]].<ref>{{Cite web|last1=DeLaura|first1=Amy|title=Republicans see Virginia's 7th District as their best chance to flip a Democratic seat in the state|url=https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/campaigns/congressional/4688151/republican-virginia-7th-district-flip-democratic-seat/|date=August 20, 2026|access-date=September 6, 2026|website=[[Washington Examiner]]}}</ref> Vindman is also a polarizing figure within his own party, as he has broken party ranks to vote with Republicans on legislation such as the [[Laken Riley Act]], the [[Protect Economic and Academic Freedom Act]], and H.R. 2616 (also known as the Stopping Indoctrination and Protecting Kids Act). These votes have drawn criticism from the [[Progressivism|progressive]] wing of the party, particularly among immigrant,<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Muzyk|first1=Cher|title=Immigrant advocates blast congressmen for supporting the Laken Riley Act|url=https://www.princewilliamtimes.com/news/immigrant-advocates-blast-congressmen-for-supporting-the-laken-riley-act/article_0067d8c4-dddb-11ef-b63d-3b7cb98b91a2.html|date=January 28, 2025|access-date=September 6, 2026|website=Prince William Times}}</ref> pro-[[Palestine|Palestinian]],<ref>{{Cite magazine|last1=Ferguson|first1=Malcolm|title=Every Democrat Who Voted to Defund Schools That Boycott Israel|url=https://newrepublic.com/post/215096/list-democrats-names-vote-defund-schools-boycott-israel-bds|date=September 3, 2026|access-date=September 6, 2026|magazine=[[The New Republic]]}}</ref> and [[Transgender rights movement|transgender]]<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Wiggins|first1=Christopher|title=Virginia congressman's meeting with transgender Democrats erupts into profanity. Then he walked out|url=https://www.advocate.com/politics/national/eugene-vindman-transgender-confrontation|date=April 12, 2026|access-date=September 6, 2026|website=Advocate}}</ref> activists. He faces Republican nominee Doug Ollivant,<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Mates|first1=Thomas|title=Doug Ollivant wins Virginia GOP Primary for US House District 7|url=https://wjla.com/news/local/doug-ollivant-wins-virginia-gop-primary-for-us-house-district-7|date=August 4, 2026|access-date=September 6, 2026|website=[[WJLA-TV]]}}</ref> who formerly served on the [[United States National Security Council]] under President [[George W. Bush]]. The race is expected to favor Vindman, as the district narrowly voted yes on the redistricting amendment; however, six candidates appear on the ballot for the general election. Including [[2024 United States presidential election|2024]] [[Constitution Party (United States)|Constitution Party]] presidential nominee [[Randall Terry]].<ref name="General"/>

A forum was scheduled to be held at the [[University of Mary Washington]]. Vindman declined to participate, while three other candidates, including Ollivant, agreed to attend. The forum was initially scheduled for September 15 but was rescheduled for the following week, on September 22. Prior to the date of the forum, Ollivant withdrew, citing personal health issues. The event was then canceled outright.<ref>{{Cite web|author=PLN News|title=UMW cancels Tuesday's 7th District debate after Ollivant drops off a slate Vindman never joined|url=https://www.potomaclocal.com/2026/09/21/umw-cancels-tuesdays-7th-district-debate-after-ollivant-drops-off-a-slate-vindman-never-joined/|date=September 21, 2026|access-date=September 23, 2026|website=Potomac Local News}}</ref> On October 5, Ollivant announced that he had been diagnosed with a treatable form of acute myeloid [[leukemia]]. Despite the cancer diagnosis, Ollivant remained committed to staying in the race.<ref>{{Cite web|author=Martin A. Davis Jr.|title=7th District Republican Candidate Doug Ollivant Has Cancer, Stays in Race|url=https://virginiafreepress.com/7th-district-republican-candidate-doug-ollivant-has-cancer-stays-in-race/|date=October 5, 2026|website=Virginia Free Press}}</ref>

{{see also|Virginia's 8th congressional district}}
The 8th district is located in [[Northern Virginia]] and includes populous cities and suburbs, such as the cities of [[Alexandria, Virginia|Alexandria]] and [[Falls Church, Virginia|Falls Church]], the entirety of [[Arlington County, Virginia|Arlington]], and parts of eastern [[Fairfax County, Virginia|Fairfax County]]. The incumbent is Democrat [[Don Beyer]], who was re-elected with 71.5% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

===Election background===
Congressman Beyer represents a safe Democratic seat and is not expected to face a serious challenge from Republicans, third parties, or independents.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Virginia 8 2026 HOUSE FORECAST|url=https://www.pollsmax.com/2026-us-house/virginia-8/forecast/|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=PollsMax.com}}</ref>

The 9th district takes in rural [[southwest Virginia]], including [[Abingdon, Virginia|Abingdon]], [[Blacksburg, Virginia|Blacksburg]], [[Bristol, Virginia|Bristol]] and [[Norton, Virginia|Norton]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Morgan Griffith]], who was re-elected with 72.5% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

Congressman Griffith represents a safe Republican seat and is not expected to face a serious challenge from Democrats.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Virginia 9 2026 HOUSE FORECAST|url=https://www.pollsmax.com/2026-us-house/virginia-9/forecast/|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=PollsMax.com}}</ref> Griffith has refused to debate his Democratic challenger Joy Powers.<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Orner|first1=Ben|title=Rep. Morgan Griffith turns down debates with 9th District challenger Powers|url=https://roanoke.com/news/local/government-politics/elections/article_53fb4685-3ddd-4e30-9ebc-ae6c2faccd62.html|date=September 6, 2026|website=[[The Roanoke Times]]}}</ref>

The 10th district is based in the [[Northern Virginia]] suburbs and exurbs, encompassing [[Fauquier County, Virginia|Fauquier]], [[Loudoun County, Virginia|Loudoun]], and [[Rappahannock County, Virginia|Rappahannock]] counties, the independent cities of [[Manassas, Virginia|Manassas]] and [[Manassas Park, Virginia|Manassas Park]], and portions of [[Fairfax County, Virginia|Fairfax]] and [[Prince William County, Virginia|Prince William]] counties. The incumbent is Democrat [[Suhas Subramanyam]], who was elected with 52.1% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

===Election background===
Congressman Subramanyam has a significant fundraising advantage and is not expected to face a serious challenge from Republicans.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Virginia 10 2026 HOUSE FORECAST|url=https://www.pollsmax.com/2026-us-house/virginia-10/forecast/|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=PollsMax.com}}</ref> Subramanyam met his Republican challenger Dave Beckwith at a debate held at [[The National Conference Center]] hosted by the Loudoun Chamber of Commerce on September 22.<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Bowman|first1=Jack|title=Rep. Subramanyam, GOP's Beckwith square off in 10th District debate|url=https://www.fauquiernow.com/news/rep-subramanyam-election-opponent-beckwith-square-off-in-debate-4ffa7c5c|date=September 22, 2026|access-date=September 23, 2026|website=FauquierNow.com}}</ref> The organizers had initially invited all three candidates. However, a day earlier, the invitation to independent candidate Ahsen Malik was rescinded. Malik alleges that his invitation was withdrawn because one of the major party nominees threatened to withdraw if he attended.<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Pampaloni|first1=Hanna|title=Independent Candidate Dropped from Chamber's Candidate Debate|url=https://www.loudounnow.com/news/independent-candidate-dropped-from-chamber-s-candidate-debate/article_14313856-a12c-4e5b-bae3-856d8f768056.html|date=September 21, 2026|access-date=September 23, 2026|website=LoudonNow.com}}</ref>

The 11th district encompasses portions of suburban [[Northern Virginia]], including the city of [[Fairfax, Virginia|Fairfax]] and portions of [[Fairfax County, Virginia|Fairfax County]]. The incumbent is Democrat [[James Walkinshaw]], who was elected with 75.1% of the vote in a [[2025 Virginia's 11th congressional district special election|special election]] to finish the term of representative [[Gerry Connolly]], who died in office on May 21, 2025.<ref name="HPVT"/> Connolly had previously announced he would not run for re-election in April, citing health concerns.

===Election background===
Congressman Walkinshaw represents a safe Democratic seat and is not expected to face a serious challenge from Republicans or third parties.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Virginia 11 2026 HOUSE FORECAST|url=https://www.pollsmax.com/2026-us-house/virginia-11/forecast/|access-date=September 5, 2026|website=PollsMax.com}}</ref>
