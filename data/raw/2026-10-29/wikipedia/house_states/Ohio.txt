{{Elections in Ohio}}
The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Ohio''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the fifteen [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|state]] of [[Ohio]], one from each of the state's [[Ohio's congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections coincide with [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, a [[2026 United States Senate special election in Ohio|special election]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. The primary elections took place on May 5, 2026.<ref>{{Cite web|date=May 9, 2025|title=2026 State Primary Election Dates|url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates|website=NCSL|access-date=August 5, 2025}}</ref>

== Background ==
===Redistricting===
{{stack|[[File:Ohio Congressional Districts, 120th Congress.svg|thumb|upright=1.36|Map of Ohio's congressional districts as passed by the Ohio Redistricting Commission on October 31, 2025. This will be used for the 2026 House elections.<ref>{{Cite news|last=Pelzer|first=Jeremy|date=October 31, 2025|title=Ohio Redistricting Commission unanimously OKs map of new congressional districts|url=https://www.cleveland.com/open/2025/10/ohio-redistricting-commission-unanimously-oks-map-of-new-congressional-districts.html|url-status=live|access-date=November 14, 2025|work=[[Cleveland.com]]}}</ref><br />{{maplink|from1=Ohio's 1st congressional district (2027–).map|from2=Ohio's 2nd congressional district (2027–).map|from3=Ohio's 3rd congressional district (2027–).map|from4=Ohio's 4th congressional district (2027–).map|from5=Ohio's 5th congressional district (2027–).map|from6=Ohio's 6th congressional district (2027–).map|from7=Ohio's 7th congressional district (2027–).map|from8=Ohio's 8th congressional district (2027–).map|from9=Ohio's 9th congressional district (2027–).map|from10=Ohio's 10th congressional district (2027–).map|from11=Ohio's 11th congressional district (2027–).map|from12=Ohio's 12th congressional district (2027–).map|from13=Ohio's 13th congressional district (2027–).map|from14=Ohio's 14th congressional district (2027–).map|from15=Ohio's 15th congressional district (2027–).map|text=Interactive map version}}]]}}

Due to constitutional requirements, Ohio needed to redraw its congressional map in time for the 2026 election, as the current map was only valid for two cycles. Republicans initially planned for a map that would have resulted in a 13 Republican to two Democrat representation in the state, netting the party three seats. In October 2025, Republicans and Democrats on the state's [[redistricting commission]] reached a deal that resulted in a map that made [[Ohio's 1st congressional district|Ohio's 1st]] and [[Ohio's 9th congressional district|9th congressional district]] more Republican-friendly. Democrats protected the Akron-area [[Ohio's 13th congressional district|13th congressional district]], which was redrawn to be slightly more friendly towards Democrats. The redrawn maps are expected to increase Republican chances to flip OH-1 and OH-9, which Republican nominee [[Donald Trump]] would have won in the [[2024 United States presidential election in Ohio|2024 presidential election]] by three and eleven points, respectively.<ref name=":6" />

The 9th district is based in [[Northwest Ohio]], including [[Toledo, Ohio|Toledo]] and the western [[Lake Erie]] coast. The incumbent is Democrat [[Marcy Kaptur]], who was reelected with 48.3% of the vote in 2024. The district was redrawn in 2025 in an effort to flip the district to the Republican Party. Despite this, Kaptur announced she would seek reelection.
