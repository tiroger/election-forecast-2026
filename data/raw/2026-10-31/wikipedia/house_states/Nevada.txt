The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Nevada''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the four [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|state]] of [[Nevada]], one from each of the state's [[Nevada's congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections will coincide with the [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate elections|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. The primary elections took place on June 9, 2026.<ref>{{Cite web |date=May 9, 2025 |title=2026 State Primary Election Dates |url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates |website=NCSL |access-date=August 5, 2025 |archive-date=November 13, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251113164508/https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates |url-status=live }}</ref>

{{see also|Nevada's 1st congressional district}}
The 1st district expands from inner [[Las Vegas]] towards its southeastern suburbs and some rural parts of [[Clark County, Nevada|Clark County]], taking in the cities of [[Paradise, Nevada|Paradise]], [[Henderson, Nevada|Henderson]], and [[Boulder City, Nevada|Boulder City]]. The incumbent is Democrat [[Dina Titus]], who was re-elected with 52.0% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT">{{cite web|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|title=2024 House Vote Tracker|access-date=February 25, 2025|website=[[The Cook Political Report with Amy Walter|Cook Political Report]]|archive-date=January 28, 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250128171130/https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|url-status=live}}</ref>

{{see also|Nevada's 2nd congressional district}}
The 2nd district includes [[White Pine County, Nevada|White Pine County]] and part of [[Lyon County, Nevada|Lyon County]], and contains the cities of [[Reno, Nevada|Reno]], [[Sparks, Nevada|Sparks]], and [[Carson City, Nevada|Carson City]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Mark Amodei]], who was re-elected with 55.0% of the vote in 2024 against an independent candidate.<ref name="HPVT" />
