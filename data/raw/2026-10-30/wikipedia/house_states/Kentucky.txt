The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Kentucky''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the six [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|state]] of [[Kentucky]], one from each of the state's [[Kentucky congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections will coincide with [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate elections|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. The primary elections took place on May 19, 2026.<ref>{{Cite web|date=May 9, 2025 |title=2026 State Primary Election Dates |url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates|website= NCSL |access-date=5 August 2025}}</ref>

The 1st district is based in Western [[Kentucky]], and stretches into Central Kentucky, taking in [[Henderson, Kentucky|Henderson]], [[Hopkinsville, Kentucky|Hopkinsville]], [[Madisonville, Kentucky|Madisonville]], [[Paducah, Kentucky|Paducah]], [[Murray, Kentucky|Murray]], and [[Frankfort, Kentucky|Frankfort]]. The incumbent is Republican [[James Comer]], who was re-elected with 74.7% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT">{{cite web|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|title=2024 House Vote Tracker|access-date=December 2, 2024 |website=[[The Cook Political Report with Amy Walter|Cook Political Report]]}}</ref>

The 3rd district encompasses nearly all of [[Louisville, Kentucky|Louisville Metro]], which, since the merger of 2003, is consolidated with [[Jefferson County, Kentucky|Jefferson County]], though other incorporated cities, such as [[Shively, Kentucky|Shively]] and [[St. Matthews, Kentucky|St. Matthews]], exist within the county. {{see also|Kentucky's 4th congressional district}}
The 4th district is located in the northeastern part of the state, along the [[Ohio River]], including the suburbs of [[Cincinnati]] and the eastern suburbs of [[Louisville, Kentucky|Louisville]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Thomas Massie]], who was re-elected unopposed in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

===Republican primary===
Thomas Massie, a [[libertarian Republican]] first elected in [[2012 United States House of Representatives elections in Kentucky#District 4|2012]], has frequently clashed with [[Donald Trump]] on fiscal and international issues both during and between his terms as president.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ackley|first=Kate|date=March 27, 2020|title=Who is Thomas Massie and why does Trump want him thrown out of GOP?|url=https://www.rollcall.com/2020/03/27/who-is-thomas-massie-the-house-member-from-kentucky-trump-wants-thrown-out-of-the-gop/|access-date=July 20, 2022|website=[[Roll Call]]|language=en|archive-date=August 22, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220822181808/https://rollcall.com/2020/03/27/who-is-thomas-massie-the-house-member-from-kentucky-trump-wants-thrown-out-of-the-gop/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=July 21, 2025 |title=Seven years of clashes define Thomas Massie's relationship with President Trump |url=https://www.kentucky.com/news/local/article311067930.html |access-date=May 13, 2026 |website=Lexington Herald Ledger}}</ref> By March 2025, Trump had openly called for a primary challenge to Massie in response to his votes on the [[2025 United States federal budget|2025 federal budget]].<ref name="massie">{{cite news |date=March 11, 2025 |access-date=March 11, 2025 |work=[[The National Desk]] |title=Thomas Massie announces reelection bid as Trump vows to back primary challenger |author=Walker, Jackson |url=https://thenationaldesk.com/news/americas-news-now/thomas-massie-announces-reelection-bid-as-trump-vows-to-back-primary-challenger}}</ref> Other high-profile clashes between the two have included Massie's vote against the [[One Big Beautiful Bill Act]], his [[Epstein Files Transparency Act|advocacy]] for the release of the [[Epstein files]], and his condemnation of [[2025 United States strikes on Iranian nuclear sites|U.S. strikes on Iranian nuclear sites]].<ref>{{cite news |date=June 22, 2025 |access-date=June 22, 2025 |work=[[Lexington Herald-Leader]] |title=KY Rep. Thomas Massie is at odds again with Trump over Iran. Here's the history |author=Horn, Austin |url=https://www.kentucky.com/news/politics-government/article309170635.html}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Goodman |first=Sylvia |date=2026-05-11 |title=Massie or Gallrein? Kentucky voters to decide the true meaning of MAGA in heated GOP primary |url=https://www.lpm.org/news/2026-05-11/massie-or-gallrein-kentucky-voters-to-decide-the-true-meaning-of-maga-in-heated-gop-primary |access-date=2026-05-13 |website=Louisville Public Media |language=en}}</ref>

On October 17, 2025, Trump endorsed farmer and retired Navy SEAL [[Ed Gallrein]] for the seat.<ref name="ballotpedia">{{Cite web |title=Ed Gallrein |url=https://ballotpedia.org/Ed_Gallrein |access-date=2026-10-07 |website=Ballotpedia |language=en}}</ref> On October 21, Gallrein announced his candidacy, challenging Massie.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Wells |first=Jeremy |date=2026-05-27 |title=President Trump wasn't about to lose in the Fourth District |url=https://cartercountytimes.com/editorials/editorial/president-trump-wasnt-about-to-lose-in-the-fourth-district/ |access-date=2026-10-07 |website=Carter County Times |language=en-US}}</ref> On May 11, 2026, the primary became the most expensive U.S. House primary in American history, with over $25.6 million in ad spending beating the prior record of $25.2 million from the [[2024 United States House of Representatives elections in New York#District 16|Democratic primary]] for [[New York's 16th congressional district]] in 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Solender |first=Andrew |date=2026-05-11 |title=Inside the wild $25 million fight to oust top GOP Trump critic Thomas Massie |url=https://www.axios.com/2026/05/11/thomas-massie-ed-gallrein-kentucky-aipac-trump |access-date=2026-05-12 |website=Axios |language=en}}</ref> By May 17, ad spending exceeded $32 million, with [[Israel lobby in the United States|pro-Israel interest groups]] accounting for over $9 million of the spending against Massie.<ref name="Record">{{cite news|last=Kashinsky|first=Lisa|title=Massie's primary is the most expensive in history. Pro-Israel groups have played a huge part.|url=https://www.politico.com/news/2026/05/17/massie-aipac-record-spending-israel-maga-trump-primary-00925375|work=Politico|date=17 May 2026}}</ref> Massie described the primary election as a "[[referendum]] on whether [[Israel]] gets to buy seats in Congress,"<ref>{{cite news|last=Scanlan|first=Quinn|url=https://abcnews.com/Politics/ahead-tuesday-primary-trump-target-massie-billionaires-buy/story?id=133046728|title=Ahead of Tuesday primary, Trump target Massie says billionaires are trying to 'buy' his seat|work=[[ABC News (United States)|ABC News]]|date=17 May 2026}}</ref> while others described it as a referendum on Trump.<ref>{{cite news|last=Smith|first=David|url=https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/may/16/kentucky-republican-primary-election-massie-gallrein|title=Can a Republican defy Donald Trump and survive? Kentucky voters will decide|work=[[The Guardian]]|date=16 May 2026}}</ref>

During the campaign, pro-Massie and pro-Gallrein PACs both used [[AI-generated content in American politics|AI-generated political ads]] to attack each other's campaigns.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.cincinnati.com/story/news/politics/elections/2026/05/16/thomas-massie-faces-ed-gallrein-kentucky-may-19-primary/90079310007/|title=The Massie-Gallrein race is not what most people think|work=Cincinnati.com|date=16 May 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last1=Sonka|first1=Joe|last2=Goodman|first2=Sylvia|url=https://www.lpm.org/news/2026-05-05/ai-deepfake-ads-attack-massie-and-gallrein-in-northern-kentucky-gop-primary|title=AI 'deepfake' ads attack Massie and Gallrein in northern Kentucky GOP primary|work=Louisville Public Media|date=5 May 2026}}</ref> One AI-generated ad from pro-Gallrein PAC MAGA KY included imagery of Massie having a [[threesome]] with pro-[[Palestine|Palestinian]] Democratic representatives [[Ilhan Omar]] and [[Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]].<ref name="Omar">{{cite news|last=Delaney|first=Arthur|url=https://www.huffpost.com/entry/thomas-massie-throuple-attack-ad-cheating-squad_n_69f8dca9e4b06e9242f57b3f|title=MAGA Attack Ad Uses AI To Show Thomas Massie 'Cheating With The Squad'|work=[[HuffPost]]|date=4 May 2026}}</ref> Massie, Omar, and former Rep. [[Marjorie Taylor Greene]] condemned the ad, with Greene accusing the ad of violating the [[TAKE IT DOWN Act]].<ref name="Omar"/> On May 16, a pro-Trump group announced that Secretary of Defense [[Pete Hegseth]] would visit Kentucky to raise support for Gallrein. Hegseth's move was accused by critics of violating the [[Hatch Act]], which the White House denied.<ref>{{cite news|last=Aitken|first=Peter|url=https://www.newsweek.com/pete-hegseth-thomas-massie-ed-gallreinand-hatch-act-violation-11961018|title=Pentagon Defends Hegseth Stumping Against Massie Amid Hatch Act Claims|work=[[Newsweek]]|date=17 May 2026}}</ref> During the campaign, Gallrein declined to debate Massie, ignored most interview requests, and conducted few public rallies.<ref name="Grayson"/><ref>{{Cite web |last=Almendarez |first=Jolene |title=The Massie-Gallrein race is not what most people think |url=https://www.cincinnati.com/story/news/politics/elections/2026/05/16/thomas-massie-faces-ed-gallrein-kentucky-may-19-primary/90079310007/ |access-date=2026-05-18 |website=The Enquirer |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Doll |first=Keely |title=Why Ed Gallrein isn't interested in debating Thomas Massie |url=https://www.courier-journal.com/story/News/politics/elections/2026/04/10/ed-gallrein-thomas-massie-debate-2026-primary-elections/89543309007/ |access-date=2026-05-18 |website=The Courier-Journal |language=en-US}}</ref>

On May 19, Gallrein obtained the Republican nomination, defeating Massie 54.9% to 45.1%. Massie conceded the election on election night, joking in his concession speech that "it took a while to find Ed Gallrein in [[Tel Aviv]]."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Nash |first=Charlie |date=2026-05-20 |title=Thomas Massie Mocks Ed Gallrein With Parting Shot After Conceding Kentucky Primary |url=https://www.mediaite.com/politics/thomas-massie-mocks-he-had-to-find-ed-gallrein-in-tel-aviv-to-concede-kentucky-primary/ |access-date=2026-05-21 |website=Mediaite |language=en}}</ref> [[AIPAC]] later sent out an announcement on X, stating that "support for America's alliance with Israel is good policy and good politics."<ref>{{Cite news |date=2026-06-03 |title=AIPAC 🇺🇸🇮🇱 (@AIPAC) on X |url=https://x.com/AIPAC/status/2062263728704266466 |access-date=2026-10-07 |work=X (formerly Twitter) |language=en-US}}</ref>

{{see also|Kentucky's 6th congressional district}}
The 6th district is located in central Kentucky, taking in [[Lexington, Kentucky|Lexington]], [[Richmond, Kentucky|Richmond]], and [[Georgetown, Kentucky|Georgetown]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Andy Barr (American politician)|Andy Barr]], who was re-elected with 63.0% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

On April 22, 2025, Barr announced that he would [[2026 United States Senate election in Kentucky|run for U.S. Senate in 2026]].<ref name="AndyBarr" />

Democrats last won this district in [[2010 United States House of Representatives elections in Kentucky|2010]], when [[Ben Chandler]] was narrowly reelected.
