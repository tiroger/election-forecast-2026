The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Washington''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the ten [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|state]] of [[Washington state|Washington]], one from each of the state's ten [[Washington's congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections will coincide with the [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate elections|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. Primary elections took place on August 4, 2026.<ref>{{Cite web|date=May 9, 2025 |title=2026 State Primary Election Dates |url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates|website= NCSL |access-date=5 August 2025}}</ref>

The 3rd district is based in [[Vancouver, Washington|Vancouver]] and encompasses Washington's portion of the [[Portland, Oregon]] metropolitan area. District 3 is the most competitive congressional district in the state, with a [[Cook PVI]] of R+2. The incumbent, Democrat [[Marie Gluesenkamp Perez]], was re-elected with 51.7% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/> In that year, she was one of only 13 Democrats to win a congressional district in a district also won by [[Donald Trump]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://centerforpolitics.org/crystalball/the-2024-crossover-house-seats-overall-number-remains-low-with-few-harris-district-republicans/|title=2024 House Vote Tracker |access-date=January 12, 2026 |website=The Center for Politics}}</ref>

Gluesenkamp Perez has received criticism from her [[Left-wing politics|left]] for her support of the [[Safeguard American Voter Eligibility Act|SAVE Act]], her vote to [[censure]] fellow Democratic Representative [[Al Green (politician)|Al Green]], and other positions.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-05-16 |title=Democratic Rep. Gluesenkamp Perez won in a Trump district. Now she faces an uprising from the left |url=https://apnews.com/article/gluesenkamp-perez-primary-democrat-election-trump-8f85d32c125c2b5f9ac40c1794fe60de |access-date=2025-09-04 |website=AP News |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|title=Angry crowd greets Gluesenkamp Perez at town hall after voter registration vote|url=https://katu.com/news/local/marie-gluesenkamp-perez-town-hall-amid-backlash-president-donald-trump-safeguard-american-voter-eligibility-act-save-act|date=April 24, 2025|work=[[KATU]]|author=Neumann, Erik}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|title=Rep. Marie Gluesenkamp Perez defends record during heated Vancouver town hall|url=https://www.opb.org/article/2025/04/25/marie-gluesenkamp-perez-vanvouver-town-hall-rep-al-green-immigrants-save-act-politics/|date=April 24, 2025|work=[[OPB]]|author=Park, Victor}}</ref> In May 2025, Brent Hennrich, a stay-at-home father and candidate for this district in [[2022 United States House of Representatives elections in Washington#District 3|2022]], announced his intention to run against Perez, citing her support for the SAVE Act and the [[Laken Riley Act]].<ref name="auto2">{{cite news |last1=Weisend |first1=Alexis |title=3rd Congressional District Democrat challenging Perez ran for seat in 2022 |url=https://www.columbian.com/news/2025/may/15/3rd-congressional-district-democrat-challenging-perez-ran-for-seat-in-2022/ |access-date=May 16, 2025 |work=[[The Columbian]] |date=May 15, 2025}}</ref>

On January 22, 2026, Representative Gluesenkamp Perez was one of seven Democrats that voted to fund the [[Department of Homeland Security]], which includes [[Immigration and Customs Enforcement]].<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2026-01-23 |title=Rep. Gluesenkamp Perez backs DHS funds amid Dem concerns on immigration policies |url=https://komonews.com/news/politics/washington-state-rep-gluesenkamp-perez-congress-backs-dhs-funds-amid-democrats-concerns-on-immigration-policies-government-shutdown-ice-renee-good |access-date=2026-10-22 |website=KOMO News |language=en}}</ref>

{{see also|Washington's 4th congressional district}}
The 4th district encompasses much of central Washington, including [[Yakima, Washington|Yakima]] and the [[Tri-Cities, Washington|Tri-Cities]] of [[Kennewick, Washington|Kennewick]], [[Pasco, Washington|Pasco]], and [[Richland, Washington|Richland]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Dan Newhouse]], who was re-elected with 52.0% of the vote against another Republican in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

Incumbent Congressman [[Dan Newhouse]] opted to retire instead of running for another term.<ref>https://newhouse.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/newhouse-announces-he-will-not-seek-reelection</ref> He initially supported Republican [[Yakima County]] Commissioner Amanda McKinney<ref name="amanda">https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/aug/19/dan-newhouse-wont-endorse-amanda-mckinney-to-succe/</ref> before switching his support to Republican State Senator [[Matt Boehnke]] after McKinney declared her opposition to Newhouse at a [[TPUSA]] rally,<ref name="amanda"/> despite supporting Newhouse in previous bids.<ref>https://www.tri-cityherald.com/news/politics-government/article315929747.html</ref>

Republican Amanda McKinney and Democrat John Duresky advanced through the August 4 primary with 34.8% and 29.9% of the vote respectively. The 3rd and 4th place finishers, Boehnke and Republican Jerrod Sessler, have refused to endorse McKinney.<ref name="amanda"/> Traditionally conservative leaning papers such as the [[Yakima Herald-Republic]]<ref name="herald"/> and the [[Tri-City Herald]] have endorsed McKinney's Democratic opponent. Congressman [[Dan Newhouse]] has declined to endorse either candidate.<ref name="amanda"/>

{{see also|Washington's 5th congressional district}}
The 5th district encompasses much of eastern Washington, including [[Spokane, Washington|Spokane]], [[Pullman, Washington|Pullman]], and [[Walla Walla, Washington|Walla Walla]]. The district has strongly favored Republicans, though Democrats were more competitive during the [[2018 United States House of Representatives elections|2018 House elections]], the first midterm elections of President [[Donald Trump]]'s first term, when Democratic nominee [[Lisa Brown (Washington politician)|Lisa Brown]] lost the district by 9.5 percentage points. The district is rated as "Safe R" by [[The Cook Political Report]].<ref>{{Cite web | title=2018 House Race ratings {{!}} The Cook Political Report | url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/ratings/house-race-ratings | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170815061710/http://cookpolitical.com/ratings/house-race-ratings | access-date=2026-08-01 | archive-date=2017-08-15}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=2018 General Election - Congressional District 5 |url=https://results.vote.wa.gov/results/20181106/us-representative-congressional-district-5.html |website=[[Washington Secretary of State]] |access-date=May 17, 2026}}</ref>

The [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|2026 House elections]] will coincide with the second midterm elections of Trump's presidency. The incumbent is Republican [[Michael Baumgartner]], who was elected with 60.6% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT" /> He is running for re-election.<ref name="Baumrun">{{Cite news |last=Cabeza |first=Garret |date=March 30, 2026 |title=Baumgartner toasts to Trump as his re-election campaign starts with big fund-raising lead |url=https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/mar/30/baumgartner-toasts-to-trump-as-his-re-election-cam/ |work=[[The Spokesman-Review]]}}</ref>
