The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Louisiana''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the 6 [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|state]] of [[Louisiana]], one from each of the state's [[Louisiana's congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections will coincide with the [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate elections|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. In races where no candidate receives over 50% of the vote, runoff elections will take place on December 12, 2026. 

Party [[primary election]]s were originally scheduled for May 16, 2026, with the potential primary runoffs scheduled for June 27.<ref>{{Cite web|date=May 9, 2025 |title=2026 State Primary Election Dates |url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates|website= NCSL |access-date=5 August 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.wwltv.com/article/news/politics/elections/us-house-primaries-officially-rescheduled-to-fall-louisiana-officials-say/289-203a2a88-808d-418a-955f-52c7bad152cc|title=U.S. House primaries officially rescheduled to fall, Louisiana officials say|publisher=[[WWL-TV]]|last=Walberg|first=Rae|date=May 14, 2026|access-date=May 16, 2026}}</ref> On April 30, Governor [[Jeff Landry]] announced that he would postpone the primaries to give state legislators time to redraw congressional maps following the [[Supreme Court of the United States|United States Supreme Court]] decision in ''[[Louisiana v. Callais]]''.<ref>{{Cite web|date=April 30, 2026 |title=Louisiana will delay House primaries after Supreme Court redistricting ruling, governor says|url=https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/louisiana-delay-house-primaries-supreme-court-redistricting-ruling-rcna342858|website= NBC News|access-date=30 April 2026}}</ref> It was then decided that they will be held using the [[Nonpartisan primary|jungle primary]] system, in which all candidates regardless of party compete on the same ballot on the date of the [[general election]], November 3. 50% of the vote is required to win the election outright; otherwise, a runoff is scheduled for December 12.<ref>{{cite web |title=AP Decision Notes: What to expect in Louisiana's state primary runoff |url=https://thehill.com/homenews/campaign/louisiana-primary-runoff-saturday-what-to-expect/#:~:text=Primaries%20for%20U.S,regardless%20of%20party. |website=The Hill |access-date=June 27, 2026}}</ref>

==Redistricting==
===Background===
In April 2026, the Supreme Court ruled on ''[[Louisiana v. Callais]]'' that stricter scrutiny had to be applied with regard to section 2 of the [[Voting Rights Act]]. The state following the ruling requested the Court strike down the lower court ruling to have two black majority districts, which it proceeded to do. The state government has pushed to move back the primary dates in light of the court requiring redistricting.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Hutchinson |first1=Piper |title=Supreme Court strikes down Louisiana congressional maps in case with national implications |url=https://lailluminator.com/2026/04/29/supreme-court-callais/ |website=Louisiana Illuminator |date=April 29, 2026 }}</ref> On May 29, 2026, Governor [[Jeff Landry]] signed a new map into law, removing one of the two plurality-Black, Democratic-controlled districts.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Saksa |first1=Jim |title=Gov. Landry signs Louisiana gerrymander into law, erasing majority-Black district |url=https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/louisiana-lawmakers-send-new-congressional-map-erasing-majority-black-district-to-governor-to-sign/ |website=[[Democracy Docket]] |date=May 29, 2026 }}</ref>
