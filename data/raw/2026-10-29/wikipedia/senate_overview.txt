The '''2026 United States Senate elections''' are scheduled to be held on November 3, 2026, with 33 of the 100 seats in the [[United States Senate|Senate]] being contested in regular elections. In these elections, voters will elect candidates to six-year terms that begin on January 3, 2027, and expire on January 3, 2033.

Senators are divided into three [[Classes of United States senators|classes]] whose terms are staggered, so that a different class is elected every two years. Class 2 senators were last elected in [[2020 United States Senate elections|2020]] and are up for election in 2026. With the [[2024 Senate Republican Conference leadership election|election]] of [[John Thune]] as leader of the [[Senate Republican Conference|Republican Conference]], this will be the first election year since [[2006 United States Senate elections|2006]] in which Senate Republicans are not led by [[Mitch McConnell]]. The [[Senate Democratic Caucus|Senate Democratic Conference]] has been led by [[Chuck Schumer]] since 2017.

[[Special elections]] have been scheduled in [[Ohio]] and [[Florida]], following the resignation of [[JD Vance]] to become [[Vice President of the United States|vice president]] and [[Marco Rubio]]'s resignation to become [[United States secretary of state]]. The appointed incumbents in these races are Republicans [[Jon Husted]] and [[Ashley Moody]], both of whom are running in their special elections to complete their terms until [[2028 United States Senate elections|2028]].

Two incumbent senators—[[Bill Cassidy]] of [[Louisiana]] and [[John Cornyn]] of [[Texas]]—lost renomination in their respective primaries, while two nominees—[[Graham Platner]] and [[Lindsey Graham]]—were replaced, respectively by a convention and a special primary, as their party's nominee in [[2026 United States Senate election in Maine|Maine]] and [[2026 United States Senate election in South Carolina|South Carolina]]. In three states ([[2026 United States Senate election in Idaho|Idaho]], [[2026 United States Senate election in Nebraska|Nebraska]], and [[2026 United States Senate election in South Dakota|South Dakota]]), the Democratic nominee has withdrawn from the race, effectively clearing the field for an independent candidate.

==Partisan composition==
All 33 [[Classes of United States senators|Class 2]] Senate seats and two [[Classes of United States senators|Class 3]] seats are up for election in 2026. Class 2 currently consists of 20 Republicans and 13 Democrats.<ref>{{cite web |title=Class II - Senators Whose Terms of Service Expire in 2027 |url=https://www.senate.gov/senators/Class_II.htm |publisher=United States Senate |access-date=January 22, 2026}}</ref> Democrats are defending two seats in states won by [[Donald Trump]] in [[2024 United States presidential election|2024]], in both cases by less than 3 percentage points: [[2026 United States Senate election in Michigan|Michigan]], where incumbent [[Gary Peters]] is not running for reelection; and Georgia, where the incumbent is [[Jon Ossoff]]. Michigan elected Democrat [[Elissa Slotkin]] to the Senate in [[2024 United States Senate election in Michigan|2024]] by 0.3 percentage points. Georgia did not have a Senate election in 2024, although in the most recent Senate election, in [[2022 United States Senate election in Georgia|2022]], Democrat [[Raphael Warnock]] won by three points in a runoff.<ref name="margin">{{Cite web |date=November 26, 2024 |title=2024 Presidential Elections By State Margin of Victory |url=https://www.foxnews.com/elections/2024/general-results/margin-of-victory |access-date=November 26, 2024 |website=Fox News Elections}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Election Results |url=https://results.sos.ga.gov/results/public/Georgia/elections/2022DecGenRun |access-date=August 4, 2025 |publisher=Georgia Secreatary of State}}</ref>

Five incumbent Democratic senators represent states won by [[Kamala Harris]] by single-digit margins in 2024: New Hampshire's [[Jeanne Shaheen]], Minnesota's [[Tina Smith]], Virginia's [[Mark Warner]], New Jersey's [[Cory Booker]], and New Mexico's [[Ben Ray Luján]].<ref name="margin" /> Shaheen and Smith are not running for reelection.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/jeanne-shaheen-wont-run-re-election-opening-another-democratic-senate-rcna196015 |title=Jeanne Shaheen won't run for re-election, opening another Democratic Senate seat in 2026 |date=March 12, 2025 |publisher=NBC News}}</ref> [[Susan Collins]]'s seat in Maine is the only seat being defended by a Republican in a state that Kamala Harris won in [[2024 United States presidential election in Maine|2024]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.politico.com/live-updates/2025/03/25/congress/susan-collins-polling-00246823 |title=Will Democrats finally get a shot at taking out Susan Collins? |date=March 25, 2025 |website=Politico}}</ref> Republican [[Thom Tillis]] of North Carolina is retiring<ref name="politico carney">{{Cite web |author=Carney, Jordain |url=https://www.politico.com/news/2025/06/29/thom-tillis-retires-00432045 |title=Thom Tillis says he will retire following Trump attacks |date=June 29, 2025 |website=Politico}}</ref> from the Senate in a state that Trump won by a single-digit margin in [[2024 United States presidential election in North Carolina|2024]].<ref name="margin" />

The 2026 Senate map is considered favorable to Republicans, even though Democrats are defending 13 seats and Republicans 22. Most rating groups identify two Republican-held seats as highly competitive and three or four others as somewhat competitive. By contrast, Democrats must flip at least four seats to win a majority while defending two highly vulnerable seats and two others viewed as somewhat vulnerable.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Why Republicans start out as favorites in the 2026 Senate elections |url=https://abcnews.go.com/538/republicans-start-favorites-2026-senate-elections/story?id=116243572 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241202212003/https://abcnews.go.com/538/republicans-start-favorites-2026-senate-elections/story?id=116243572 |url-status=dead |archive-date=December 2, 2024 |access-date=February 6, 2025 |publisher=ABC News |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Berman |first=Russell |date=May 12, 2025 |title=Retirement Is the New Resistance |url=https://www.theatlantic.com/politics/archive/2025/05/democrats-age-retirement-trump/682771/ |access-date=May 13, 2025 |website=The Atlantic |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date= April 24, 2025 |title=Senate Democratic retirements clear the way for a new generation: From the Politics Desk |url=https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/politics-news/senate-democratic-retirements-clear-way-new-generation-politics-desk-rcna202678 |access-date=May 13, 2025 |publisher=NBC News |language=en}}</ref> Pollsters have found Democrats' chances to flip the Senate to be improving due to nominees in states such as [[2026 United States Senate election in North Carolina|North Carolina]], [[2026 United States Senate special election in Ohio|Ohio]], [[2026 United States Senate election in Texas|Texas]], [[2026 United States Senate election in Kansas|Kansas]], and [[2026 United States Senate election in Alaska|Alaska]] running highly competitive campaigns, and because of [[Donald Trump]]'s unpopularity amid the [[2026 Iran war]].<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/07/11/here-are-races-watch-democrats-grow-more-bullish-retaking-senate/ |title=Democrats are growing bullish on retaking the Senate. ===Defeats===
Two Republicans sought reelection, but lost in the primary election. The last elected incumbent senator to lose renomination was [[Richard Lugar]] in [[2012 United States Senate election in Indiana|2012]],{{efn|name=PrimaryLoss|Republican senator [[Luther Strange]] ran to complete the term he was appointed to in [[2017 United States Senate special election in Alabama|2017]], but lost the primary to [[Roy Moore]], who narrowly lost the general election to Democratic nominee [[Doug Jones (politician)|Doug Jones]].}} and the last time multiple elected incumbent senators lost renomination in primaries was in [[2010 United States Senate elections|2010]].{{efn|Two elected incumbent senators lost renomination in primaries in 2010: [[Lisa Murkowski]] in [[2010 United States Senate election in Alaska|Alaska]] and [[Arlen Specter]] in [[2010 United States Senate Democratic primary election in Pennsylvania|Pennsylvania]]. After losing her primary, Murkowski ran a write-in campaign during the general election and won. In addition, [[Bob Bennett (politician)|Bob Bennett]] lost renomination at the state convention in [[2010 United States Senate election in Utah|Utah]].}} The last incumbent senator to place no better than third in a primary before Cassidy was [[Hattie Caraway]] in [[1944 United States Senate election in Arkansas|1944]].<ref name="SittingSenatorsPlaceThird">{{cite web|url=https://smartpolitics.lib.umn.edu/2026/02/09/how-often-do-sitting-us-senators-place-third-or-worse-in-primaries/|title=How Often Do Sitting US Senators Place Third (Or Worse) in Primaries?|publisher=Smart Politics|date=February 9, 2026|access-date=February 14, 2026}}</ref>{{efn|Republican senator [[Maurice J. == Predictions ==

Multiple sites and political pundits have published predictions of competitive seats. These predictions have considered factors such as the strength of the [[incumbent]] (if the incumbent was running for reelection) and the other candidates, and the state's partisan lean, reflected in part by the state's [[Cook Partisan Voting Index]] rating. The predictions typically assign ratings to each seat, indicating the predicted advantage that a party had in winning that seat. Most election predictors use:
* "<u>tossup</u>": no advantage
* "<u>tilt</u>" (used by some predictors): advantage that is not quite as strong as "lean"
* "<u>lean</u>": slight advantage
* "<u>likely</u>": significant, but surmountable, advantage
* "<u>safe</u>" or "<u>solid</u>": near-certain chance of victory<!-- Colors and sorting:

In many cases, the incumbent has yet to say their intention of running for another term. If an incumbent intends to retire then place "<br />{{Small|(Retiring)}}" after the incumbent's name.

==Potentially competitive seats==
These are seats that polling and predictions currently have listed as at least somewhat close.

===Republican incumbents with Democratic challengers===
One [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican]]-held senate seat, [[2026 United States Senate election in North Carolina|North Carolina]], is considered competitive but favoring the Democrats.<ref name=":0" /> Former [[Governor of North Carolina|Governor]] [[Roy Cooper]] overwhelmingly won the Democratic nomination for the open seat.<ref name="nc">{{Cite web |date=July 28, 2025 |title=Roy Cooper, former NC Governor, announces he is running for Senate in 2026 |url=https://abc11.com/post/roy-cooper-us-senate-former-north-carolina-governor-announces-bid/17274141/ |access-date=July 28, 2025 |publisher=ABC11 Raleigh-Durham |language=en}}</ref> Incumbent Republican senator [[Thom Tillis]] is retiring,<ref name="Tillis">{{cite web |url=https://www.politico.com/news/2025/06/29/thom-tillis-retires-00432045 |title=Thom Tillis says he will retire following Trump attacks |last=Carney |first=Jordain |date=June 29, 2025 |access-date=June 29, 2025 |website=[[Politico]]}}</ref> with former [[Republican National Committee]] Chairman [[Michael Whatley]] becoming the Republican nominee, endorsed by [[Donald Trump]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=December 2, 2025 |title=Michael Whatley officially enters North Carolina Senate race, endorsed by Trump |url=https://wlos.com/news/local/michael-whatley-officially-enters-north-carolina-senate-race-endorsed-president-donald-trump-usa-politics-government-roy-cooper-republican-gop-state-board-elections/ |access-date=February 12, 2026 |publisher=ABC13 Asheville |language=en}}</ref>

Six Republican-held Senate races are expected to be highly competitive: [[2026 United States Senate election in Maine|Maine]], [[2026 United States Senate special election in Ohio|Ohio]], [[2026 United States Senate election in Alaska|Alaska]], [[2026 United States Senate election in Texas|Texas]], [[2026 United States Senate election in Iowa|Iowa]], and [[2026 United States Senate election in Kansas|Kansas]]. Incumbent [[Susan Collins]] is seeking reelection in Maine;<ref name="el">{{Cite web |last=Altimari |first=Mary Ellen McIntire and Daniela |date=January 7, 2025 |title=An early look at potentially vulnerable senators in 2026 |url=https://rollcall.com/2025/01/07/senate-vulnerable-2026-midterm-electio/ |access-date=June 5, 2025 |website=Roll Call |language=en-US}}</ref> it is regarded as competitive due to the [[Cook Partisan Voting Index|state's blue lean]], but Collins kept her seat during cycles favorable to Democrats nationally ([[2008 United States Senate election in Maine|2008]] and [[2020 United States Senate election in Maine|2020]]). Military veteran [[Graham Platner]] was the Democratic nominee in Maine until he withdrew from the race due to allegations of sexual assault. He was replaced by former [[Maine Senate]] president [[Troy Jackson]].<ref>{{Cite web |title= Maine - Election Results 2008 - The New York Times |url= https://archive.nytimes.com/www.nytimes.com/elections/2008/results/states/maine.html |access-date= June 5, 2025 |website= The New York Times}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date= November 3, 2020 |title= Maine U.S. Senate Election Results |url= https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2020/11/03/us/elections/results-maine-senate.html |access-date= June 5, 2025 |work= The New York Times |language= en-US |issn= 0362-4331}}</ref> Incumbents in Ohio ([[Jon Husted]]) and Alaska ([[Dan Sullivan (U.S. senator)|Dan S. Sullivan]]) are expected to have competitive races against former U.S. senator [[Sherrod Brown]] and former U.S. representative [[Mary Peltola]], respectively.<ref name="el" /> Both [[2024 United States Senate election in Ohio|Brown]] and [[2024 United States House of Representatives election in Alaska|Peltola]] lost reelection in 2024. In Texas, incumbent Senator [[John Cornyn]] lost the Republican primary election to [[Texas Attorney General]] [[Ken Paxton]], who is expected to have a competitive race against [[Texas House of Representatives|State Representative]] [[James Talarico]]. In Iowa, the incumbent [[Joni Ernst]] is not seeking reelection;<ref name="ernst" /> the race to succeed her between Republican U.S. representative [[Ashley Hinson]] and Democratic state representative [[Josh Turek]] is expected to be competitive. In Kansas, the gap between the Democratic nominee, pastor [[Adam Hamilton (pastor)|Adam Hamilton]], and incumbent Senator [[Roger Marshall]] narrowed in early October polls.   

Four other Republican-held Senate races are expected to favor Republicans over Democrats but are viewed as somewhat competitive by some predictors: [[2026 United States Senate special election in Florida|Florida]], [[2026 United States Senate election in Mississippi|Mississippi]], [[2026 United States Senate election in South Carolina|South Carolina]], and [[2026 United States Senate election in Louisiana|Louisiana]]. In Louisiana, incumbent Senator [[Bill Cassidy]] was challenged by [[Louisiana State Treasurer]] [[John Fleming (Louisiana politician)|John Fleming]] and U.S. representative [[Julia Letlow]], with Letlow winning the primary election, marking the first time an elected incumbent senator had lost their primary in any state since [[2012 United States Senate election in Indiana|2012 in Indiana]].{{efn|name=PrimaryLoss}}

===Republican incumbents with independent challengers===
Four Senate races with Republican incumbents have [[independent politician|independent]] candidates as the main challengers to the Republicans: [[2026 United States Senate election in Nebraska|Nebraska]], [[2026 United States Senate election in Idaho|Idaho]], [[2026 United States Senate election in Montana|Montana]], and [[2026 United States Senate election in South Dakota|South Dakota]]. In Nebraska, Idaho, and South Dakota, the Democratic candidates dropped out of the race.<ref>{{cite news |last= Goldmacher |first= Shane |title= Democrat Quits Senate Race in South Dakota, Clearing Way for Independent |newspaper= The New York Times |date= August 4, 2026 |url= https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/04/us/politics/julian-beaudion-drops-out-south-dakota.html |access-date = October 12, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web| url= https://www.politico.com/news/2026/08/04/south-dakota-democrat-julian-beaudion-withdraws-01024746| title= South Dakota Democrat exits Senate race, clears way for independent| date= August 4, 2026| website= Politico| last= Howard| first= Andrew}}</ref> In Nebraska, incumbent Senator [[Pete Ricketts]] is challenged by independent [[Dan Osborn]], who ran against Republican [[Deb Fischer]] in the [[2024 United States Senate election in Nebraska|Class I]] race in 2024. Osborn was the most successful challenger of a Republican-held seat during the [[2024 United States elections|2024 election cycle]], losing by only 6.7 points, compared to [[Kamala Harris]]'s loss by 20.5 points in Nebraska in the [[2024 United States presidential election in Nebraska|concurrent presidential election]]. In August 2026, the Nebraska Democratic Party endorsed Osborn.<ref>{{Cite web |last= Coblerobler |first= Ariana Joy |date= August 1, 2025 |title= Dan Osborn endorsed by NE Democratic Party for 2026 Senate race |url= https://nebraska.tv/news/local/dan-osborn-endorsed-by-ne-democratic-party-for-2026-senate-race |access-date= November 16, 2025 |publisher= NTV}}</ref>

===Democratic incumbents===
The [[Democratic Party (United States)|Democratic]]-held Senate race expected to be the most competitive is in [[2026 United States Senate election in Michigan|Michigan]], where senator [[Gary Peters]] has announced his retirement. The state has an even [[Cook Partisan Voting Index|PVI]] score.<ref>{{Cite web |date= January 28, 2025 |title= Michigan Democratic Sen. Gary Peters will not run for reelection, opening a key Senate seat in 2026 |url= https://apnews.com/article/gary-peters-michigan-retirement-72fb02bbc816e31f035d797f9185599c |access-date=June 5, 2025 |publisher=Associated Press |language=en|author1=Cappelletti, Joey |author2= Volmert, Isabella}}</ref><ref name= "el" /> Former US Representative for the [[Michigan's 8th congressional district|8th congressional district]] and [[2024 United States Senate election in Michigan|2024]] U.S. Senate nominee [[Mike Rogers (Michigan politician)|Mike Rogers]]<ref name= "MRogers" /> is the Republican nominee. Former [[Wayne County, Michigan|Wayne County]] Health, Human, and Veterans Services Department Director [[Abdul El-Sayed]]<ref name= "El Sayed" /> is the Democratic nominee. In [[2026 United States Senate election in New Hampshire|New Hampshire]], where incumbent Democratic senator [[Jeanne Shaheen]] is retiring, the race is also expected to be competitive. U.S. Representative [[Chris Pappas (American politician)|Chris Pappas]] is the Democratic nominee and former U.S. Senator [[John E. Sununu]] is the Republican nominee. Sununu lost to Shaheen in [[2008 United States Senate election in New Hampshire|2008]].<ref name= "JohnSununu" /> Some polls have shown the race as a dead heat,<ref>{{Cite web |url= https://www.nrsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/new-hampshire-poll.pdf |title= NH Senate Race is a Dead Heat |first= Erik| last= Iverson |work= Peak Insights |date= July 9, 2026 |access-date= July 10, 2026}}</ref><ref name= "EmersonNHMarch2026">{{cite web |title= New Hampshire 2026: Sununu Leads GOP Nomination, Ties Pappas for Senate |url= https://emersoncollegepolling.com/new-hampshire-2026-sununu-leads-gop-nomination-ties-pappas-for-senate/ |publisher= Emerson College Polling |date= March 26, 2026 |access-date= July 19, 2026}}</ref> with two August polls showing Sununu ahead.

In [[2026 United States Senate election in Georgia|Georgia]], incumbent Democratic senator [[Jon Ossoff]] is running against Republican [[Mike Collins (politician)|Mike Collins]], the [[U.S. representative]] from {{ushr|GA|10}}.<ref>{{cite news |department= AJC Politics |access-date= July 18, 2025 |title= U.S. Rep. Mike Collins prepares Senate run in Georgia, aiming to seize MAGA lane |date= July 18, 2025 |url= https://www.ajc.com/politics/2025/07/us-rep-mike-collins-prepares-senate-run-in-georgia-aiming-to-seize-maga-lane/ |work= [[The Atlanta Journal-Constitution]] |author= Bluestein, Greg}}</ref> The race was initially expected to be very competitive,<ref name= "el" /> but election predictors have become increasingly confident of Ossoff's prospects.<ref>{{cite web |url= https://thehill.com/homenews/campaign/5999066-ossoff-georgia-sabatos-crystal-ball-uva/ |last= Mancini |first= Ryan |date= July 30, 2026 |access-date= August 5, 2026|website= The Hill |title= Ossoff reelection bid, 5 House seats move toward Democrats: Sabato's Crystal Ball}}</ref>

{{See also|List of United States senators from Alabama}}
Republican [[Tommy Tuberville]], who was elected in [[2020 United States Senate election in Alabama|2020]] with 60.1% of the vote, initially planned to seek reelection. But on May 27, 2025, he announced that he would instead run for [[2026 Alabama gubernatorial election|governor of Alabama]].<ref name= "o472">{{cite web |last= Angle |first= Alex |title= Tuberville makes it official: he's running for governor in 2026 |website= Alabama Daily News |date= May 27, 2025 |url= https://aldailynews.com/tuberville-to-announce-run-for-governor-in-2026/ |access-date= September 17, 2026}}</ref>

Held on May 19, the Republican primary featured U.S. Representative [[Barry Moore (American politician)|Barry Moore]],<ref>{{cite news |last1= Markus |first1= Nicole |title= Barry Moore announces run for Senate in Alabama |url= https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/politics/barry-moore-announces-run-for-senate-in-alabama/ar-AA1KnUn7?ocid=hpmsn&cvid=ff3c61adbcd9466394dd1eee9be38080&ei=13 |access-date= August 12, 2025 |work= Politico |date= August 12, 2025}}</ref> former [[Navy SEAL]] Jared Hudson,<ref>{{cite news |last1= Thomas |first1= Erica |title= 'Fighter for Alabama': Former Navy SEAL Jared Hudson running for U.S.… |url= https://1819news.com/news/item/fighter-for-alabama-former-navy-seal-jared-hudson-running-for-u-s-senate |access-date= May 28, 2025 |work= 1819 News |date= May 28, 2025}}</ref> and [[Attorney general of Alabama|state attorney general]] [[Steve Marshall]].<ref name= "MarshallAL">{{cite web |last1= Baker |first1= Trent |title= 'The nation needs strong conservative leadership': Steve Marshall announces U.S. Senate bid |url= https://1819news.com/news/item/the-nation-needs-strong-conservative-leadership-steve-marshall-announces-us-senate-bid |website= 1819 News |access-date= May 29, 2025}}</ref> Since no candidate reached 50%, a runoff was held on June 16 between the top two finishers. Moore won the runoff, becoming the Republican nominee.
