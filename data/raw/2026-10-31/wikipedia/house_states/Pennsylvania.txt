{{Elections in Pennsylvania}}
The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Pennsylvania''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the 17 [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|State]] of [[Pennsylvania]], one from each of the state's [[Pennsylvania's congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections will coincide with [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate elections|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. [[Primary election|Primary elections]] took place on May 19, 2026.<ref>{{Cite web |date=September 26, 2025 |title=2026 State Primary Election Dates |url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates |access-date=September 27, 2025 |publisher=National Conference of State Legislatures}}</ref>

{{see also|Pennsylvania's 1st congressional district}}
The 1st district is based in the northern suburbs of [[Philadelphia]], including all of [[Bucks County, Pennsylvania|Bucks County]] and parts of [[Montgomery County, Pennsylvania|Montgomery County]].<ref name="2022dists" /> The incumbent is Republican [[Brian Fitzpatrick (American politician)|Brian Fitzpatrick]], who was re-elected with 56.4% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT">{{cite web|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|title=2024 House Vote Tracker|access-date=December 2, 2024 |website=[[The Cook Political Report with Amy Walter|Cook Political Report]]}}</ref> Fitzpatrick, a [[Moderate Republicans (modern United States)|moderate]], was expected to face a primary challenge from a more right-wing candidate after voting against the [[One Big Beautiful Bill Act]], but ultimately drew no challengers.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Otterbein |first1=Holly |title=This Pennsylvania Republican withstood pressure on the megabill. Here's why. |url=https://www.politico.com/news/2025/07/03/brian-fitzpatrick-trump-megabill-obbba-00439788 |access-date=July 7, 2025 |work=[[Politico]] |date=July 3, 2025}}</ref>

===Democratic primary===
[[Chris Rabb]], a [[Democratic socialism|democratic socialist]], won the primary, defeating healthcare official [[Ala Stanford]] and state senator [[Sharif Street]], a member of an influential Philadelphia political family. Rabb ran as the most left-wing candidate in the race and received support from members of the [[Squad (U.S. Congress)|Squad]], a group of progressive members of Congress.<ref name=":0">{{Cite news |last=Kashinsky |first=Lisa |date=May 19, 2026 |title=Progressive firebrand Chris Rabb wins Democratic primary for the nation’s bluest House seat |url=https://www.politico.com/news/2026/05/19/rabb-wins-pennsylvania-house-primary-00929091 |access-date=June 8, 2026 |work=[[Politico]]}}</ref> Stanford was endorsed by outgoing representative Evans.<ref name="JTA">{{Cite news |last=Strauss |first=Joseph |date=May 19, 2026 |title=With AOC backing and anti-Israel message, Chris Rabb wins primary in Philadelphia |url=https://www.jta.org/2026/05/19/politics/with-aoc-backing-and-anti-israel-message-chris-rabb-vies-for-open-house-seat-in-philadelphia |access-date=June 8, 2026 |work=[[Jewish Telegraphic Agency]]}}</ref>

During the campaign, Rabb accused the pro-Israel lobbying group [[AIPAC]] of indirectly supporting Stanford by funneling money through [[314 Action]], an organization that supports candidates with [[Science, technology, engineering, and mathematics|STEM]] backgrounds. Stanford, 314 Action, and AIPAC denied the allegation. 314 Action spent approximately $2.5 million in support of Stanford.<ref name=":2">{{Cite news |last=Cerino |first=Marco |date=April 6, 2026 |title=Third District congressional race intensifies amid PAC money disputes, endorsements and escalating political attacks |url=https://www.phillytrib.com/news/local_news/third-district-congressional-race-intensifies-amid-pac-money-disputes-endorsements-and-escalating-political-attacks/article_db6e52e8-59a7-4506-a65f-0cb2e68d825e.html |access-date=June 8, 2026 |work=[[The Philadelphia Tribune]]}}</ref> Rabb [[Gaza genocide|described the war in Gaza as a genocide]] and called on his opponents to do the same.<ref name=":0" /> [[American Priorities]], a pro-Palestinian super PAC established to counter AIPAC's influence, spent $400,000 in support of Rabb.<ref name="JTA" />

===Green Party===
Andrew Tupone filed nomination papers with nearly 11,000 signatures to appear on the ballot as the [[Green Party of Pennsylvania|Green Party]] candidate, though the party disavowed his candidacy and said he did not represent it. Tupone, a former Republican, changed his registration to Green in April 2026. Democrats accused him of running as a [[spoiler candidate]] to siphon votes from Bob Brooks, noting that he was represented by a Republican-affiliated attorney and that several of his petition circulators were Republicans. [[Commonwealth Court of Pennsylvania|Commonwealth Court]] Judge [[Stella Tsai]] ruled that Tupone was ineligible to appear on the ballot under the Green Party label because the party had not selected him as its candidate.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Baruchman |first1=Michelle |last2=Janesch |first2=Sam |title=Green Party candidate Andrew Tupone is removed from ballot in Lehigh Valley congressional race |url=https://www.inquirer.com/politics/pennsylvania/green-party-seventh-district-congress-lehigh-valley-ballot-election-20260904.html |access-date=5 September 2026 |work=The Philadelphia Inquirer |date=September 4, 2026}}</ref> Tupone appealed to the [[Pennsylvania Supreme Court]] which upheld the ruling.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ulrich |first=Steve |date=September 17, 2026 |title=Pennsylvania Supreme Court Says No To Andrew Tupone in PA-07 |url=https://politicspa.com/pennsylvania-supreme-court-says-no-to-andrew-tupone-in-pa-07/153407/ |access-date=2026-09-17 |website=PoliticsPA |language=en-US}}</ref> He has said he intends to mount a [[write-in campaign]].<ref>{{cite news |last1=Karbal |first1=Ian |title=Judge rules Green Party candidate accused of being GOP plant can be kicked off ballot in PA-07 |url=https://penncapital-star.com/briefs/judge-rules-green-party-candidate-accused-of-being-gop-plant-can-be-kicked-off-ballot-in-pa-07/ |access-date=5 September 2026 |work=Pennsylvania Capital-Star |date=September 4, 2026}}</ref>

The 9th district is based in North Central Pennsylvania east of the [[Eastern Continental Divide|Appalachian Divide]].  The 9th district includes Susquehanna, Bradford, Columbia, Lebanon, Montour, Northumberland, Schuylkill, Sullivan & Wyoming counties, as well as part of Berks, Luzerne, & Lycoming; this includes Williamsport, Bloomsburg and Lebanon. The incumbent is Republican [[Dan Meuser]], who was re-elected with 70.5% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT" />

The 10th district is based in the [[Harrisburg, Pennsylvania|Harrisburg]] and [[York, Pennsylvania|York]] areas, including all of [[Dauphin County, Pennsylvania|Dauphin County]], most of [[Cumberland County, Pennsylvania|Cumberland County]], and the northern half of [[York County, Pennsylvania|York County]].<ref name="2022dists" /> The incumbent is Republican [[Scott Perry]], who was re-elected with 50.6% of the vote in 2024.
