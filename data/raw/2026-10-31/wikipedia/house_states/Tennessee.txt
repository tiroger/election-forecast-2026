The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Tennessee''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the nine [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|State]] of [[Tennessee]], one from all nine of the state's [[Tennessee congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections will coincide with [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate elections|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. Primary elections took place on August 6, 2026.<ref>{{Cite web|date=May 9, 2025 |title=2026 State Primary Election Dates |url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates|website= NCSL |access-date=5 August 2025}}</ref>

==Redistricting==
===Background===
On May 6, 2026, Tennessee Republicans released a newly proposed congressional map that would draw out Rep. [[Steve Cohen (politician)|Steve Cohen]] (D-TN) of Memphis, shifting the state's delegation from an 8–1 Republican majority to a 9–0 Republican majority. The map passed both chambers and was signed into law by [[Governor of Tennessee|governor]] [[Bill Lee (Tennessee politician)|Bill Lee]] on May 7.<ref>{{Cite web|date=May 7, 2026 |title=Tennessee's governor signs into law a new US House map that carves up a majority-Black district in Memphis
 |url=https://www.kare11.com/article/syndication/associatedpress/tennessees-governor-signs-into-law-a-new-us-house-map-that-carves-up-a-majority-black-district-in-memphis/616-e31187fc-5c28-4368-9a64-7845a3857b1f|website= Kare 11|access-date=7 May 2026}}</ref> The enactment of the new map extended the congressional filing deadline to May 15.<ref>{{cite web |title=How to Run for the United States House of Representatives |url=https://sos-prod.tnsosgovfiles.com/s3fs-public/document/US%20House%202026%20-%20Special%20Qualifying%20Period.pdf |website=[[Tennessee Secretary of State]] |access-date=7 May 2026}}</ref>

The 1st district is based in northeast Tennessee, encompassing all of [[Carter County, Tennessee|Carter]], [[Cocke County, Tennessee|Cocke]], [[Greene County, Tennessee|Greene]], [[Hamblen County, Tennessee|Hamblen]], [[Hancock County, Tennessee|Hancock]], [[Hawkins County, Tennessee|Hawkins]], [[Johnson County, Tennessee|Johnson]], [[Sullivan County, Tennessee|Sullivan]], [[Unicoi County, Tennessee|Unicoi]], and [[Washington County, Tennessee|Washington]] counties and parts of [[Jefferson County, Tennessee|Jefferson]] and [[Sevier County, Tennessee|Sevier]] counties, and includes the [[Tri-Cities, Tennessee|Tri-Cities]] region. The incumbent is Republican [[Diana Harshbarger]], who was re-elected with 78.1% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT">{{cite web|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|title=2024 House Vote Tracker|access-date=December 2, 2024 |website=[[The Cook Political Report with Amy Walter|Cook Political Report]]}}</ref>

The 5th district comprises a southern portion of [[Davidson County, Tennessee|Davidson County]]; portions of [[Wilson County, Tennessee|Wilson]] and [[Williamson County, Tennessee|Williamson Counties]]; and the entirety of [[Maury County, Tennessee|Maury]], [[Lewis County, Tennessee|Lewis]], and [[Marshall County, Tennessee|Marshall Counties]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Andy Ogles]], who was re-elected with 56.9% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/> Ogles was challenged by Tennessee Agricultural Commissioner Charlie Hatcher. Hatcher won the primary with 53% of the vote, defeating Ogles.

The 6th district encompasses the eastern portions and suburbs of [[Nashville]] and extends across northern [[Middle Tennessee]]. It includes the cities of [[Hendersonville, Tennessee|Hendersonville]] and [[Gallatin, Tennessee|Gallatin]], as well as the eastern portion of [[Lebanon, Tennessee|Lebanon]]. The district continues eastward across the [[Cumberland Plateau]], including the regional centers of [[Cookeville, Tennessee|Cookeville]] and [[Crossville, Tennessee|Crossville]], and reaches as far as [[Scott County, Tennessee|Scott County]], where [[East Tennessee]] begins.

The incumbent is Republican [[John Rose (Tennessee politician)|John Rose]], who was re-elected with 68.0% of the vote in 2024;<ref name="HPVT" /> he is retiring to run for governor.<ref name=":0" />

[[File:Tennessee's 7th congressional district (since 2027) (new version).svg|thumb|Tennessee's 7th congressional district boundary from the 2026 elections<br>{{maplink|from=Tennessee's 7th congressional district (2027–).map|text=Interactive map version}}]]
The 7th district is centered in [[Middle Tennessee]], anchored by significant portions of [[Nashville]] and its western suburbs. The district includes most of [[Franklin, Tennessee|Franklin]] as well as the western half of [[Williamson County, Tennessee|Williamson County]], along with nearby communities such as [[Ashland City, Tennessee|Ashland City]], [[Pleasant View, Tennessee|Pleasant View]], [[Dickson, Tennessee|Dickson]], [[Springfield, Tennessee|Springfield]], and part of [[White House, Tennessee|White House]].

To the northwest, the district prominently includes [[Clarksville, Tennessee|Clarksville]], one of the state's largest cities anchored by [[Fort Campbell]].

Beyond that the district stretches southward to the [[Alabama]] border, encompassing a large swath of predominantly rural counties.

The incumbent is Republican [[Matt Van Epps]], who won a [[2025 Tennessee's 7th congressional district special election|special election]] following the resignation of Republican [[Mark Green (Tennessee politician)|Mark Green]] with 53.9% of the vote.
