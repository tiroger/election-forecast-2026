The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Georgia''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the 14 [[United States House of Representatives|U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|state]] of [[Georgia (U.S. state)|Georgia]]. The elections will coincide with [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate elections|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. Primary elections took place on May 19, 2026, and in races where no candidate received over 50% in a primary, runoff elections were held on June 16.<ref name="sp26">{{Cite web|date=May 9, 2025 |title=2026 State Primary Election Dates |url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates|website= NCSL |access-date=5 August 2025}}</ref>

The 1st district is based in the southeast corner of the state, encompassing [[Savannah, Georgia|Savannah]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Buddy Carter]], who was re-elected with 62.0% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/> On May 8, 2025, Carter announced that he would [[2026 United States Senate election in Georgia|run for U.S. Senate]] in 2026.<ref name="BuddyCarter">{{cite news |last1=Bluestein |first1=Greg |title='MAGA warrior' Buddy Carter jumps into Georgia Senate race against Ossoff |url=https://www.ajc.com/politics/maga-warrior-buddy-carter-jumps-into-georgia-senate-race-against-ossoff/ZBEW6UFRA5DUBAKNSL2GM2GTSQ/ |access-date=May 8, 2025 |work=The Atlanta Journal-Constitution |date=May 8, 2025 |language=English}}</ref>

{{see also|Georgia's 13th congressional district}}
The 13th district is based in the southwest suburbs and exurbs of [[Atlanta, Georgia|Atlanta]]. The incumbent is Democrat [[Everton Blair]] who was elected in a [[2026 Georgia's 13th congressional district special election|special election]] after congressman [[David Scott (Georgia politician)|David Scott]] died in April 2026. Scott was re-elected with 71.8% of the vote in 2024 and was running for re-election until his death in April 2026.<ref name="HPVT">{{cite web|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|title=2024 House Vote Tracker|access-date=December 2, 2024 |website=[[The Cook Political Report with Amy Walter|Cook Political Report]]}}</ref>

Scott faced criticism from fellow Democrats due to his age; he would have been 81 years old at the beginning of the next Congress, as well as reports from his voting record revealing that he had not cast a single vote in the last six election cycles, including the [[2024 United States presidential election]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Rashid |first=Hafiz |title=80-Year-Old Swing State Democrat Didn't Vote in Past Six Elections |url=https://newrepublic.com/post/202831/80-year-old-swing-state-democrat-didnt-vote-elections-david-scott |access-date=November 7, 2025 |work=[[The New Republic]] |date=November 6, 2025 |language=English}}</ref> Scott faced several primary challengers, but stated he was running for re-election to a 13th term in Congress.<ref name="Scott13">{{cite news |last1=Solender |first1=Andrew |title=Scoop: House Democrats' oldest members mostly running again despite youth revolt |url=https://www.axios.com/2025/05/15/house-democrats-age-members-reelection-biden |access-date=May 14, 2025 |work=[[Axios (website)|Axios]] |date=May 14, 2025 |language=en}}</ref> Scott died on April 22, 2026. His name remained on the Democratic primary ballot, but votes for him were not counted in the official tally.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Homan |first=Alander Rocha, Maya |date=2026-04-23 |title=Scott’s rivals take a break from politics after the longtime congressman’s death upends the election • Georgia Recorder |url=https://georgiarecorder.com/2026/04/23/scotts-rivals-take-a-break-from-politics-after-the-longtime-congressmans-death-upends-the-election/ |access-date=2026-06-19 |website=Georgia Recorder |language=en-US}}</ref>

{{see also|Georgia's 14th congressional district}}
The 14th district is based in the northwest corner of the state. Republican [[Marjorie Taylor Greene]] was re-elected with 64.4% of the vote in 2024,<ref name="HPVT"/> but she resigned on January 5, 2026. 

A [[2026 Georgia's 14th congressional district special election|special election]] was held with district attorney [[Clay Fuller]] winning the election in a runoff and serving the remainder of the term.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-04-07|title=2026 Georgia U.S. House Nonpartisan Special Runoff Election Election Live Results|url=https://apnews.com/projects/elections-2026/georgia-special-general-runoff-results-us-house-district-14/|access-date=2026-04-08|website=AP News|language=en}}</ref>
