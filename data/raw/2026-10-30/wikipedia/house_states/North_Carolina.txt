The '''2026 United States House of Representatives elections in North Carolina''' will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the 14 [[U.S. representative]]s from the [[U.S. state|state]] of [[North Carolina]], one from each of the state's [[North Carolina's congressional districts|congressional districts]]. The elections will coincide with [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|other elections]] to the House of Representatives, [[2026 United States Senate election in North Carolina|elections]] to the [[United States Senate]], and various [[2026 United States elections|state and local elections]]. The primary elections took place on March 3, 2026, and if there was races where no candidate receives over 30% in a primary, runoff elections would take place on May 12.<ref>{{Cite web|date=May 9, 2025 |title=2026 State Primary Election Dates |url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2026-state-primary-election-dates|website= NCSL |access-date=5 August 2025}}</ref>

==Redistricting==
===Background===
On October 13, 2025, the [[North Carolina General Assembly]] announced a plan to pass a new congressional map to help the GOP maintain its control in the [[United States House of Representatives]] for the [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections|2026 elections]] as part of the broader [[2025–2026 United States redistricting|2025 U.S. redistricting cycle]] after [[Texas]] and [[Missouri]] had also passed new maps.<ref>{{cite news |title=North Carolina GOP announce plans to vote on new House map amid nationwide redistricting battle|website=[[Associated Press]]|date=October 13, 2025 |url=https://apnews.com/article/north-carolina-congress-redistricting-house-b32b6b1c52d9f59efac41c3b8c0db971}}</ref>

On October 21, 2025, the [[North Carolina Senate]] passed Senate Bill 249, which targeted the [[North Carolina's 1st congressional district|1st]] district, the state's only competitive district, as well as the more heavily [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican]]-leaning [[North Carolina's 3rd congressional district|3rd]] district, with a party-line vote of 26-20. It passed in the [[North Carolina House of Representatives|House]] on October 22, 2025, with a party-line vote of 66-48, approved by the [[North Carolina General Assembly|General Assembly]] without the signature of Governor [[Josh Stein]], who does not have the authority to veto the congressional [[North Carolina's congressional districts|map]]. This is the first time in the modern era that North Carolina has redrawn maps mid-decade without being court-ordered to do so.<ref>{{cite news |title=Senate Bill 249 / SL 2025-95 Realign Congressional Districts 2025.|website=[[North Carolina General Assembly]]|date=October 22, 2025 |url=https://www.ncleg.gov/BillLookup/2025/S249}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=North Carolina Republicans pass new congressional maps aimed at creating 11-3 majority|website=[[The Daily Tar Heel]] |date=October 23, 2025 |url=https://dailytarheel.com/article/city-ncga-republicans-redrawing-congressional-maps-20251023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=North Carolina Republicans pass new congressional maps aimed at creating 11-3 majority|url=https://dailytarheel.com/article/city-ncga-republicans-redrawing-congressional-maps-20251023}}</ref>

===Legal challenges===
On October 31, 2025, the [[North Carolina]] State Conference of the [[naacp|NAACP]], [[Common Cause]], and individual plaintiffs filed motions to seek an injunction against the newly enacted congressional map to block its use for the [[2026 United States House of Representatives elections]], arguing that the new map linked to SB 249 violates multiple [[List of amendments to the Constitution of the United States|amendments]] and targets "the heart of the [[Black Belt in the American South|Black Belt]]." It was already challenged in its prior configuration as intentionally discriminatory, exacerbating the already-demonstrated vote dilution in [[North Carolina's 1st congressional district|NC-01]] by massively reducing the district’s [[African Americans|Black]] voting-age population and eviscerating [[North Carolina]]’s longstanding [[Black Belt in the American South|Black Belt]] district. An injunction was set for the November 19th deadline.<ref>{{cite web |title=Challengers seek injunction against new NC congressional map|url=https://www.carolinajournal.com/challengers-seek-injunction-against-new-nc-congressional-map/ |website=www.carolinajournal.com|publisher=[[Carolina Journal]]|date=December 13, 2025|access-date=July 11, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=IN THE UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT FOR THE MIDDLE DISTRICT OF NORTH CAROLINA|url=https://acrobat.adobe.com/id/urn:aaid:sc:US:fd0ddd9d-030e-48e0-96b7-2a7b8c52647f}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT MIDDLE DISTRICT OF NORTH CAROLINA|url=https://acrobat.adobe.com/id/urn:aaid:sc:US:114b15e2-523a-49b4-8ff0-a88c27b021eb}}</ref>

A three-judge panel later rejected the injunction to pause the implementation of the new map, ruling that the plaintiffs had not made a clear showing that they are likely to succeed on the merits of any of the claims advanced in their preliminary injunction motions and that they had not shown that their [[First Amendment to the United States Constitution|First Amendment]] claims and [[Fourteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution|Fourteenth Amendment]] [[Partisan (politics)|partisanship]] claim are likely to be justiciable, much less successful. The panel added that the direct evidence shows that [[Partisan (politics)|partisanship]], not race, motivated the reconfiguration of the districts, and that even if the new districts have a disparate impact on [[African American|Black]] voters, that doesn’t mean lawmakers acted with discriminatory intent.<ref>{{cite news |title=Judges uphold North Carolina map for use in 2026 elections|website=[[Courthouse News Service]]|date=November 26, 2025 |url=https://www.courthousenews.com/judges-uphold-north-carolina-map-for-use-in-2026-elections/}}</ref>

In January 2026, the plaintiffs dropped the lawsuit.<ref>{{cite news |title=NC congressional map critics drop federal lawsuits|website=[[Carolina Journal]] |date=January 20, 2026 |url=https://www.carolinajournal.com/nc-congressional-map-critics-drop-federal-lawsuits/}}</ref>

[[File:North Carolina's 1st congressional district (since 2027) (new version).svg|thumb|North Carolina's 1st congressional district boundary from the 2026 elections<br/>{{maplink|from=North Carolina's 1st congressional district (2027–).map|text=Interactive map version}}]]
The 1st district was redrawn on October 22, 2025 to favor Republicans, now including all of [[Beaufort County, North Carolina|Beaufort]], [[Bertie County, North Carolina|Bertie]], [[Camden County, North Carolina|Camden]], [[Carteret County, North Carolina|Carteret]], [[Chowan County, North Carolina|Chowan]], [[Craven County, North Carolina|Craven]], [[Currituck County, North Carolina|Currituck]], [[Dare County, North Carolina|Dare]], [[Edgecombe County, North Carolina|Edgecombe]], [[Gates County, North Carolina|Gates]], [[Halifax County, North Carolina|Halifax]], [[Hertford County, North Carolina|Hertford]], [[Hyde County, North Carolina|Hyde]], [[Martin County, North Carolina|Martin]], [[Nash County, North Carolina|Nash]], [[Pamlico County, North Carolina|Pamlico]], [[Pasquotank County, North Carolina|Pasquotank]], [[Perquimans County, North Carolina|Perquimans]], [[Tyrrell County, North Carolina|Tyrell]], [[Vance County, North Carolina|Vance]], [[Warren County, North Carolina|Warren]], and [[Washington County, North Carolina|Washington]] counties, as well as a small portion of eastern [[Granville County, North Carolina|Granville County]]. The incumbent is Democrat [[Don Davis (North Carolina politician)|Don Davis]], who was re-elected with 49.5% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT">{{cite web|url=https://www.cookpolitical.com/vote-tracker/2024/house|title=2024 House Vote Tracker|access-date=February 26, 2024 |website=[[The Cook Political Report with Amy Walter|Cook Political Report]]}}</ref>

The election was one of the most expensive congressional races in the 2026 elections, with over $38.8 million in spending.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Mutnick|first=Ally|date=October 6, 2026|title=The blockbuster House races|url=https://punchbowl.news/article/campaigns/blockbuster-house-races/|access-date=2026-10-15|website=Punchbowl News|language=en-US}}</ref>

{{see also|North Carolina's 11th congressional district}}
The 11th district includes all of [[Avery County, North Carolina|Avery]], [[Buncombe County, North Carolina|Buncombe]], [[Cherokee County, North Carolina|Cherokee]], [[Clay County, North Carolina|Clay]], [[Graham County, North Carolina|Graham]], [[Haywood County, North Carolina|Haywood]], [[Henderson County, North Carolina|Henderson]], [[Jackson County, North Carolina|Jackson]], [[Macon County, North Carolina|Macon]], [[Madison County, North Carolina|Madison]], [[McDowell County, North Carolina|McDowell]], [[Mitchell County, North Carolina|Mitchell]], [[Swain County, North Carolina|Swain]], [[Transylvania County, North Carolina|Transylvania]], and [[Yancey County, North Carolina|Yancey]] counties, as well as portions of [[Polk County, North Carolina|Polk County]]. The incumbent is Republican [[Chuck Edwards]], who was re-elected with 56.8% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/> 

On August 5, 2026, after being recommended for [[Censure in the United States|censure]] by the [[House Ethics Committee]], Edwards ended his re-election bid.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Santaliz |first=Kate |date=2026-08-05 |title=Rep. {{see also|North Carolina's 13th congressional district}}
The 13th district includes all of [[Caswell County, North Carolina|Caswell]], [[Franklin County, North Carolina|Franklin]], [[Harnett County, North Carolina|Harnett]], [[Johnston County, North Carolina|Johnston]], [[Lee County, North Carolina|Lee]], and [[Person County, North Carolina|Person]] counties, as well as most of [[Granville County, North Carolina|Granville County]] and portions of [[Wake County, North Carolina|Wake County]].  The incumbent is Republican [[Brad Knott]], who flipped the district and was elected with 58.6% of the vote in 2024.<ref name="HPVT"/>

The 14th district includes all of [[Burke County, North Carolina|Burke]], [[Cleveland County, North Carolina|Cleveland]], [[Gaston County, North Carolina|Gaston]], and [[Rutherford County, North Carolina|Rutherford]] counties, as well as portions of [[Mecklenburg County, North Carolina|Mecklenburg]] and [[Polk County, North Carolina|Polk]] counties.
